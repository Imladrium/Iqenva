The Velsari are the second–most populous sentient species on Iqenva, dwelling in the shallows of most of the world's [[Sunside]] oceans and seas. They have a few various forms, but the ones most associated with the term "Velsari" often conduct trade and diplomacy with the [[Reithi]], in some cases even forming countries with them.


# Etymology
The term "Velsari" is an [[Ancurāna|Ancu]] exonym, comprised of the Ancu terms "Vēlu" and "Saryê" meaning "Water" and "Fair" plus the suffix "-i" indicating agency in a similar manner to English's "-er" suffix. Together this roughly translates to "The fair ones of the water".

# Origin
The origin of the Velsari is heavily debated amidst the Reithi, unsure of how such an obviously unnatural species could have developed. Therefore they can only deduce it to be some form of magic, potentially related to an isolated [[Catenas|Catena]] that has since been lost. Among the Velsari—however—there are multiple origin stories, which can vary wildly but most have a few common themes. Old texts and oral stories have mentioned something known as "The Resolution," originating from maritime cultural groups in northern Iqenva. From these groups arose the first Velsari, in southeastern [[Anselesna]]. From the waters there they migrated across the Ausavčāl and Trâívan Oceans, but scarcely beyond into colder waters.

In truth, their "Resolution" was a forced decision to the island–dwellers of Haqalu, during the [[War of Wings]]. When Anselesna was going to conquer the islanders, one of the High Felari intervened and offered her blessing. Those who accepted became the first Velsari.

# Appearance
They appear as ordinary Reithi, but waist-down with scaled tails (along with webbed hands, sharper teeth, and membraned ears they appear largely analogous with our concept of merfolk. They can breathe both air and water, and hold their breath for several hours. They have gills on their necks, but the size of their gills compared to their bodies means that they alone cannot provide the oxygen needed to breathe, thereby necessitating supplementary air every day or so. With Aquamancy, their time underwater can be extended moderately - but not indefinitely.

Velsari tails vary in size, shape, colour, and pattern - largely dependent on genetics, although there are some environmental factors. Velsari tails are very saturated in colour, and are patterned with tints or shades of their base hue - the hue may change subtly along the length of the tail and into the fin. Rarely, iridescence can occur - the rarest hue, however, is green. The most common depends on location, but is generally purple, cyan, and yellow. Neutral colours never occur. The ordinary skin of Velsari is far more water resistant than that of their Reithi counterparts, and tends to skew on the side of being paler due to the reduced UV necessitating less melanin underwater.

# Habits
Technological development of the Velsari is, naturally, far more limited than that of the Reithi. While they can progress and use fire / forges above-ground and use corals, it is difficult for them to build the infrastructure above ground / on floating platforms to permit these developments. The Velsari usage of Aquamancy is one of the few areas they know more about the world than the Reithi, and a major reason (along with environmental factors) why the Reithi haven’t simply subjugated the Velsari into various states of vassalage.

The Velsari use a great number of coral-like species, selectively bred over time for durability and fast growth, for things like armour, weapons, jewellery, and even building structures. Corals have—to a certain extent—replaced metals, although metals are still used as a more expensive alternative, seen as highly prestigious. Weapons are often made of obsidian, bone, rock, or (increasingly) selectively-bred corals.

Velsari clothing tends to be very flowing and lightweight, covering the torso and sometimes the upper tail, but often not the arms and lower tail.

Velsari writing systems are often based on ropes with rocks tied into them - this eventually morphed into symbols that eventually started being etched into strong, large kelp-like leaves with natural, waterproof inks.

Food tends to consist of marine plants, and various marine animals. Most is prepared and eaten raw, but some foods are cooked above the surface and eaten cooked / dried - or in geologically-active areas on the seabed. Velsari teeth are sharper and more pointed than the Reithi's, or humans.

The Velsari have a different throat structure for underwater communication. Their lungs, larynx, and pharynx are the same, but they have a smaller version of what dolphins have—a chamber that allows them to trill, whistle, and click underwater. Velsari languages rendered in air are similar to their aquatic counterparts, but with extremely different phonologies. They're approximations, at best. As such, every phoneme has two versions: the underwater version (most often a combination of clicks and / or whistles), and the terrestrial version. Being "bilingual" in this sense is often not necessary in more reclusive Velsari areas, but any presence of trade with Reithi—or the inhabitance of any Chudari—means it is highly likely most of the population will be able to speak in air.

# Chudari
It is possible for Reithi and Velsari to have children, which often leads to children with some aquatic features, like membraned ears, water resistant eyes & skin, and a few have the breath capacity of the Velsari—but most have legs, not tails. The largest concentration of hybrids (known as "Chudari" or "The Dual Ones" are in southeastern [[Nulâašilu]], and [[Sastranivê]], especially [[Acatūn]], a country with both Reithi and Velsari citizens on islands and the surrounding waters. [[Cí'Ilyān]] also has a lesser number of them, as does [[Iŋatra]]. Chudari are sought for their Aquamancy, sometimes illegally. With Aquamancy, Reithi can breathe water for a few days at a time, and adapt their bodies to mitigate decompression sickness. Specialized wheelchairs and [[Literamantic|Literamancy]] inventions allow for Velsari to visit the land not using Aquamancy, at least in countries that allow their presence.
