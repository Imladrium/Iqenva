The Reithi are the most populous sentient species of [[Iqenva]], inhabiting all continents; concentrated on the [[Sunside]], primarily in lowland regions. They are also the most technologically advanced of the three sentient species, but with different regions at different levels of development and attitudes towards industrialization. They share similar physical traits with their kin the [[Velsari]], but not the [[Felari]].


# Etymology
The term "Reithi" comes from the [[Ancurāna|Ancu]] term "Reit" which means "(To) Craft" plus the suffix "-i" indicating agency in a similar manner to English's "-er" suffix. Hence, Reithi can be translated to "Crafters" or "The People who Craft."

# Origin
Reithi are thought to originate from southeastern Anselesna, but rapidly spread south to Ŋašilu and east to [[Istarthu]] with the advent of early ship technologies. They are mostly human in appearance, but adapted to Iqenva's ecosystems and different biochemistry, the latter of which especially indicates that they most likely not related to humans. Having faced setbacks at the eruption of a supervolcano that resulted in a prolonged volcanic winter early on reducing their numbers, a more even distribution ensued and encouraged migration further from the epicentre of the devastation in Anselesna.

# Appearance
Holistically there is relatively little difference between humans and Reithi, but there are some notable disparities. They have 6 digits per limb, not 5. In terms of height they are generally taller than Humans - they can range anywhere from 5'10" to 7'4" feet tall on average, with females on average 3-6 inches shorter than males; this height is a result of the lower gravity. Most are lithe by our standards. Their bodies are also lighter than ours; their bones and tissues are far more lightweight, to account for this. Skin, and eye colours have similar ranges (eye colours are somewhat more saturated), and red eyes are more common due to [[Starside]] albinism. Facial / body hair is non-existent. Heterochromia is quite common, but more prevalent in certain areas over others.

Reithi "hair" generally interlocks in a similar manner to the barbules of feathers, is often spiky, and lays quite flat and straight (however, there is variation). This is due to the semi–avian nature of the Reithi. It would be inaccurate to call it hair, and also inaccurate to call it feathers - it is neither. It can be a multitude of colours, but there are trends as to the colour based on ancestry and geographical distribution. For instance, Starside Reithi almost uniformly have white or greyish-white hair, because of their widespread albinism.

As with Humans, these characteristics are not evenly distributed. Many Redlanders have extremely dark skin, for example, on account of sun exposure. Orangelanders often have darker or tanned skin, Bluelanders generally have fair skin, Blacklanders typically have pale skin, and Starsiders have extremely pale, albino-like skin, with very little hair & eye pigmentation.

# Habits
On account of the lower surface gravity, the thicker atmosphere, and the strong winds, in conjunction with the lithe, slightly avian, and lightweight anatomy of the Reithi, many use artificial wings as a method of transportation. Self-propelled flight is common for many journeys. Hollow bones, strong arms, a shifted centre of mass compared to our own, a somewhat different arm anatomy, etc. aids the capability of flight with constructed wings. Most cultures tend to eat more meat than us.

Reithi, along with most animal species on Iqenva, do not sleep as we do. They rest one hemisphere of their brains at a time, somewhat like dolphins on Earth. Their circadian rhythms are not based on day-night cycles for obvious reasons, rather their internal body clock, which is more malleable than our own. As such, there is a period of people half-sleeping one hemisphere of their brain, (during which they are semi–conscious, but it is still customary to sleep in a bed) then a period of wakefulness, followed by a period of people half-sleeping for the other hemisphere of the brain, then more wakefulness. A working "day" is considered 24 hours, and with around 6 hours for resting one hemisphere and 18 hours of wakefulness. However, it is notable that while it is common to divide the day into 24ths, their [[Ancu Calendar|day is equivalent to 24.93 Earth hours]]; as such, their hours, minutes, and seconds are slightly longer than hours proportionally.

Reithi generally perceive colour differently than us. Humans are able to see different shades of green the best out of all the colours, out of an evolutionary need to see better into our green foliage to hunt and avoid being hunted. However, the 4 main foliage colours on Iqenva has made this difficult to adapt to in the same way, putting the species at a slight disadvantage compared to more localized life. What occurred for most species that cross vegetation belts, including Reithi, was the evolution of different genes activating in response to the primary colours the Reithi grew up seeing, based on vegetation. For most of childhood, Reithi have statistically worse vision overall compared to adults, particularly regarding colour. Through adolescence, the number of cones and rods in their eyes adapt to suit the environment,  and their vision improves. Their vision will focus on one colour - like how our eyes are focused on green, their eyes may focus on red, orange, blue, or black. The Reithi will still be able to see the other colours—they are not colourblind to them—but nevertheless perceive colour differently compared to those of different vegetation belts. But it's important to note that this trait is not extremely altering. If growing up in a place without much vegetation, it is likely the eyes will adjust to whatever colour the terrain is If growing up between vegetation belts, their vision may focus between the two colours. The most extreme changes occur in low light levels: if growing up in a low–light environment, like the the outer Blackbelt or the Starside, the number of rods will increase in lieu of cones, providing superior night vision at the expense of better colour vision, and also meaning that when travelling sunward they must take precautions to protect their very light-sensitive eyes. The opposite is true for the sunward belts. For instance, an Orangebelter visiting the outer Blackbelt will more often-than-not become disoriented in the black forests and lower light levels, unable to see as well as native Blackbelters and wildlife. 

Reithi and [[Velsari]] have a hybrid species, known as the [[Velsari#Chudari|Chudari]].
