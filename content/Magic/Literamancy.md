Literamancy is the most widely–employed, researched, and accessible [[Catenas|Catenic Art]] of [[Iqenva]], having permeated most [[Reithi]] countries to a point of near–ubiquity. Irreducible symbols representing certain operators and words are written according to a very specific syntax within what is called a "glyph"; each result has some output associated with what was written in the glyph, which can physically affect the world in a reproducible, specific way. Glyphs may interact with one another for computations, communications, extending ranges, etc.

# Etymology
Literamancy is the general name applied to any of the arts associated with the 11 Literamantic Catenas. In [[Iŋatra|Iŋatre]] the arts are collectively referred to as "Ðalţasnâ" meaning "Magic Symbols." Other languages generally confer this meaning to it also.

# The Literamantic Catenas
While there are many types of lesser Catenas on Iqenva, the most famous and important ones all relate to Literamancy, of which there are 11 dispersed across the planet, classifying them as major Catenas.

Each Literamantic Catena is immovable relative to the planet, and each appears as an ornately carved pedestal of unbreakable and immovable black stone, around 1.6m tall, surmounted by three thin floating 11-rayed stars, intersecting each other orthogonally, emitting a pale light and rotating slowly. The light's hue depends on the Catena; each is unique.

The ground around a Catena has no bearing upon it; for example, the Great Earthquake of Ráemar moved the island westwards by approximately a metre, but the Catena remained fixed relative to its previous location. The room was redesigned to recentre the Catena once more.

The Catenas are what dictate the laws of Literamancy, but each has an effective range. Some are more advanced than others, providing a greater ability for glyphs within their ranges to effectuate their coded changes upon reality. The efficacy of glyphs decreases the further one gets from a Catena, but the prevalence of Catenas upon Iqenva means that the planet is covered with enough spheres of influence such that glyphs work all over the planet, albeit to different capacities.

# Literic Oils
The Literamantic Catenas are not only responsible for the efficacy of glyphs, but the supply of power to them. Their influence on the planet also 'leaks' what might be considered a form of energy across their spheres of influence (called Literic energy), which is technically capable of powering glyphs, only within extreme proximity to the Catenas. However, certain lifeforms have taken up the ability to refine this energy, and use it as a food source, often microbial life that mutualistically inhabits a specific plant alike to how algae may inhabit a coral. These microbes often supplement the plant's growth, allowing them to grow far faster than other comparable plants.

Often, this process is nascent in catenas in general, but the effect Literamantic catenas have over the growth of organisms is unusually high. This results in a side effect where growing up or inhabiting the area near a catena can result in becoming more powerful, called "attuning" a catena. However, this has withdrawal symptoms, as the auxillary fuel source for your body is taken away if you move further from it. In extreme cases this can result in death.

The Reithi managed to domesticate some of these specialized plants, breeding them to encourage the production of 'Literic Oils,' which have the Literic energy condensed down into a liquid applicable to glyphs. For all except the cities directly surrounding Catenas, Literic Oils are needed to perform Literamancy with glyphs. There are different formulations from different plants and microbes, native to different areas. Trade secrets are highly guarded for the production of certain oils, each specializing a certain way; be it the range a glyph can be from the Catena, the efficacy of the glyph, etc. Most oils take the form of translucent liquids slightly thicker than water, of a few varying colours dependent upon the formulation.

# Glyph Writing and Appearance
Glyphs are written using any form of indelible ink most commonly, but glyph patterns may also be imprinted into metals and cast with molds. These are more temperamental, but should they be made rightly more durable than ink–based glyphs. The medium of a glyph does not matter, it is the precision to which a glyph is drawn. Over time, the substance comprising the glyph will wear and break down, depending on the age, amount of usage, type of glyph, power, material, quality, and a myriad of other factors.

Glyphs have many shapes and may be written at any size, but all glyphs radiate outwards, often (but not always) as a spiral, with the symbols comprising the glyph read in a outwards from the starting point of the glyph at the centre. Most glyphs are densely–packed spiral shapes, with the symbols incorporated into the arms as it trails outwards. The exact type of spiral can vary according to the glyph's function.

Glyphs when inactive do not emit any light, but when activated may glow in any colour and intensity, dependent upon the glyph.

Glyphs can be programmed to have different activation conditions: such as being touched, another glyph nearby, periodically, etc. Most glyphs are touch–based.

For storage, most glyphs are kept as either paper sheets, or in books dedicated to holding them. It is customary to use the space around a glyph to describe its function and purpose, and is encouraged for the safe usage of glyphs in many regions.

# Limitations of Literamancy and Glyphs
The more powerful the glyph, the more often it has to be recharged. The more durable the base the glyph is written on or embedded into, the longer it takes to break. The better the Literic Oil and / or proximity to a Literamantic Catena, the more powerful the glyph—without affecting how often it has to be recharged.

Affected area (or volume) and complexity of the glyph (measured in number of instructions) are the two most important statistics for determining the base power of a glyph, and by extension how often it must be recharged. Since area / volume are both exponential compared to radius, power increases exponentially—and recharges required also increases exponentially. 

If the glyph doesn't affect area, then this is ignored. Some glyphs can have dynamic areas and volumes. For instance, if a glyph moves the user up (a commonly used glyph in flying) then the glyph is written to affect the user's volume, and then the user drags other things (like clothing and items they might be carrying) up with them. Any glyph applied to the user works this way. It affects the user's volume, and then the user can in turn affect other things. However, complexity is linear, and therefore recharges increase linearly when complexity increases. It is a delicate balancing act, creating glyphs that are both useful and effective, whilst also not requiring constant recharging. 

Much of the time of literamancists is spent attempting to improve existing glyphs, rather than the invention of new ones. As such, many glyphs that were once considered complex are now written with relative ease.

Glyphs cannot create and cannot destroy. They can only affect what is already present; nor can glyphs cannot alter the physical, mental, or emotional state of a person.

# Glyph Examples
Say you want to have liquid water appear before you. One would have to use a glyph that specifies the following sequence:

> The activation of the glyph → specifying a given spherical radius for the glyph to cast its effect → specifying where the centre of the sphere is, relative to the glyph and its orientation → sorting out all water molecules in a gaseous state within the radius → transporting of all the water molecules to the centre of the sphere → the condensation of all the water vapour → the ending of the glyph.

This would be an average glyph, in complexity, and would involve around a hundred strokes of ink. It would require recharging every 5-7 uses (with the average oil), and would break after perhaps 800 uses (provided it is written on paper in a book). This particular glyph needs to be recharged often because of the large area affected.

Say you wish to be teleported precisely 10 metres in the direction the glyph is facing. One would have to use a glyph that specifies the following sequence:

> The activation of the glyph → specifying that the user is affected → specifying that the glyph can affect other things the user is touching / secured to, provided they are below a certain mass (to prevent the movement of inadvertent items) → specifying the function of the glyph is teleportation → designating the distance → designating that the direction of the glyph is the desired direction → detecting whether there is anything that could interfere with the teleportation → if there is, the distance is adjusted to avoid the obstacle, the previous step occurs again, this loop happens until the teleportation is valid → the teleportation → the end of the glyph.

This is also an average glyph, with around 90 strokes of ink. It requires recharges every 27-35 uses (with the average oil and providing the user is of average size), and would break after around 800 uses (provided it is written on paper in a book).

# Literamancy in Law and Culture

Glyph reproduction, usage, and experimentation is regulated by different countries differently. However, the standard is that glyph experimentation is strictly permitted unto only literamancists who have completed standardized exams focusing on memorization, calligraphy, knowledge of the glyphs, their theory, and their application. Glyph reproduction is also allowed to the wards, while stencilling basic glpyhs may—nation dependent—be allowed to the public.

To become a literamancist in the 5 [[Ancurāna|Ancu Realms]] for example, one must first be a citizen of one of the 5 countries (or the condominium of [[Dānura]]), be the equivalent of 22 years old or older, have no criminal record, and with certain grades in the education system. This means they must be able to afford the education system. Then they must apply and be accepted to a Literamancy institute, take the equivalent of 2 years of theory, pass, and then become a ward of a literamancist. Then, after the equivalent of 5.5 years of further learning as a ward, they may graduate, take the final exam, and be granted the status of a literamancist.

Glyph usage is not often over–regulated, the primary exceptions being illegal glyphs. Examples of these include ones creating infernos, explosions, altering the atmosphere in harmful ways, etc. Some more autocratic countries, however, ban glyphs to all but the nobility in order to forcefully control the masses, and literamancists are watched carefully. The most occult and unknown glyph type is the "subcatenic glyph" whereby a glyph acts as its own miniature catena. These are poorly understood, and extremely dangerous.

There are a few literacracies (meaning: Rule of the literamancists) in Iqenva, systems of government where the literamancists form the state and use their monopoly on glyphs to control the masses. However, these are few and far between, after several revolutions against them in different countries. In most places, the literamancists are both held in high esteem and also feared by the government because of the threat they could pose, and once did. Hence, for a state to survive, it needs the loyalty of the military, the bureaucratic system, and the literamancists. There are few literamancists compared to other occupations, and many governments have taken precautions against the literamancists attempting to gain power. Most notably, literamancists are barred from any governmental roles in the Ancu Realms, and Ancu governments all watch the literamancist guilds extremely carefully.

# Speculative Forms of Literamancy

There are apparently other, more mysterious ways to write glyphs and interact with them. These are collectively known as "highglyphs".

- "Lightglyphs" are glyph symbols conjured into the air—levitating and glowing, by unknown methods. Their sightings are the most common out of all the highglyphs, although it is possible to use regular glyphs to create light patterns that resemble glyph patterns, so this is expected. Real lightglyphs, however, are elusive and mysterious.

- "Fractalglyphs" which are glyphs nested inside of other glyphs. As per the known laws of Literamancy, these should be impossible, yet a few cases have popped up in history where fractalglyphs are the only explanation for certain events. They may be related to the fact that glyphs can have loops, and can follow flowchart-like procedures.

- "Teleglyphs" are glyphs that seemingly ignore distance and / or affected-area restraints. Out of all the highglyphs, these are the most feared, given their hypothetically omniscient presence. They are also the most esoteric.

- "Autoglyphs" are glyphs that need no fuel to run. These are the least likely to exist, as they break not only the laws of Literamancy, but also natural laws of the Universe (instead of modifying them as the Catenic Arts all appear to do).

- "Innateglyphs" are glyphs that you can use with but a thought, powered by your caloric intake or by catenic proximity, and do not break. These would be exceptionally powerful, granting people powers that could be unexplainable otherwise. Innate teleportation over short distances is the one most commonly reported, but subcatenic glyphs would result in people gaining unusual powers. These are the most likely highglyph to exist. They would revolutionize aquamancy, seeing as the glyphs would be considered part of a living being—therefore the two magic systems could theoretically mix—leading to unknown, powerful, and revolutionary applications.

How some of these would be powered is a mystery. Some disregard these arts as mere myths and legends, seeing them as impossible. Some see a few of the above as fiction, and others as real. Combinations of highglyphs are even more unknown.
