Perenxše Hasya is a religion widespread in northeastern [[Nulâašilu]] and eastern [[Cresanu]], following tenets derived from the laws that Asxiŷr the Counsellor implemented in the ancient Xšaŧa Empire during the Wars of Wings.

# Etymology
Perenxše was the Xšaŧan name of Asxiýr, while "Hasya" is the genitive case of "Hasr" which means "to revere", indicating devotion towards Asxiýr. Adherence of this creed are known as the Perēni.

# History
Ever since the War of Wings, the region that is now Gixanu has followed a Felaric religion, which is a group of religions that venerates the [[Felari]]. In the case of Gixanu, they follow the Creed of Perenxše (Perenxše Hasya), and the main reverence goes to Asxiýr, who was seen as the Counsellor of emperors for hundreds of years. 

Perenxše Hasya began as a series of moral obligations stemming from laws implemented under Asxiýr, as part of his assistance from guild infighting while [[Anselesna]] was raiding the north of Telcensā. 

The Xšaŧan emperor's monk brother at the time, Casuya of Lanavāz, codified the laws of Asxiŷr into a compendium, and used it as justification for following the wisdom of Asxiýr. This was not new, as Asxiýr already had the emperor's ear, but religious undertones became to slowly intrude upon what was once a legal matter. Casuya reasoned that if Qiriýtaq was worshipped in Anselesna, and Asxiýr was the nemesis of Qiriýtaq, that Asxiýr was equal to Qiriýtaq and therefore also worthy of worship. 

Asxiŷr never desired worship or the deification that followed, having only intervened to keep Qiriýtaq from taking over Iqenva. However, he did see it as a useful tool by which he could further steer Xšaŧa against Qiriýtaq, and did not object to the movement that took root in the courts of Xšaŧa.


# Beliefs
Asxiýr long attempted to cultivate the morals of the Xšaŧa Empire as he thought a people ought to be, internally conflicted about his principles of non-interventionism but also wishing to keep them free of Qiriýtaq. Over time he became more pragmatic and comfortable with advising the Reithi, to the point of acting as the emperor in all but name. That said, the emperor was a largely spiritual and ceremonial office, and did not exert direct control over the guilds nor the civil government.

There have been branches in the religion, but not to a major extent, and often along political lines: The largely homogenous religion split when Acašyu supplanted Xšaŧa, but little changed except for who controlled the temple hierarchy. A new hierarchy set up in Êîlur and Salenuž, and Telcensā in modern times has blended the ideals of Perenxše Hasya with more local religions near the border of Brilnaxa.

Xšaŧa always placed heavy emphasis on music as a cultural tradition and pillar, to the point of some leitmotifs and chords representing words and ideas in the Xšaŧan language. As such, when Casuya set about codifying the morals of Asxiýr he composed them into a series of songs. There were six of these, and they form the main tenets of the religion. There is no holy scripture, rather the Six Songs and other supporting themes that add further detail to the history of Xšaŧa at the time. There is no set order nor ranking of the Songs. 

The songs use prosaic and antiquated ideas that have different meanings ascribed to them as the Xšaŧan language evolved and was supplanted by Gixanê, while the Songs remained set in stone. The Songs use the idea of journeys to convey their morals.

### The Six Songs Rendered in English

- And Perenxše Declared: Those who seek the summit of Qirhesyāz, bestow no ware unto your company you would not fly with.
	- Qirhesyāz is an old and important lookout over northern Telcensā in the War of Wings, used symbolically as an arduous journey's destination. This Song is similar to the Golden Rule found in many other cultures, speaking of not forcing others into tasks you would not do yourself - even in the hardest of circumstances, as Qirhesyāz in the first War of Wings was the site of a years-long siege and a symbol of resistance against Qiriýtaq. 
  
- And Perenxše Declared: Those who seek the Xţāmxta Namû, recall the Ceyanamû and your true charges.
	- This Song had large implications for the political system of ancient Xšaŧa. The Xţāmxta Namû is the compound around the Catena, in which the most senior guild and government members deliberate. The Ceyanamû (Wall of the Namû) separates one of the worst slums of the city from the pinnacle of Xšaŧan society. The Song compels the Guildmembers (and indeed everyone) to remember those lesser than them, and that their charge is meant to be looking after their citizens - not themselves or riches.

- And Perenxše Declared: Those who seek the Catavčāl, see your folk weeping at the pier; hear the wails of your home!
	- The Catavčāl is the Xšaŧan name of the [[Sunpole|Sunstorm]], far in the Ausavčāl Ocean; it represents a dangerous destination to go to out of a vain lust for glory, or headfoolishness. Leaving one's family for self interest and vanity was often shunned in ancient Xšaŧan society, where community and family were a very important part of life, especially in rural areas. This does not extend to journeys of necessity or ones for the benefit of others, most notably.

- And Perenxše Declared: Those who seek the Path of Vaŧuš shall venture down the Ālu; it is the charge of all to aid those lost in the mountains.
	- This Song is wrapped in symbolism, and often debated. Vaŧuša is a city in modern-day [[Halevnê]], used as a representation of the plateau's raiders - commonly feared in Xšaŧa. The Path of Vaŧuš is thus applied as a term more broadly for any who commit murder, theft, rape - any major criminal act. The Ālu is one of the main rivers that flows from Halevnê in Xšaŧa, and symbolizes a gateway to civilization. (It was thought that the thinner air pressure affected the sanity of people in the plateau.) Effectively the Song is about forgiveness, helping those who are lost in the ways of debauchery and sin in all walks of life, and being open to others joining the community.

- And Perenxše Declared: Those who seek the doab of Važrac, be just and be swept not into the torrents about your halls.
	- The city of Važrac is the capital of Ŋeša, the southern region of Xšaŧa around the Xšaŧây River; it is viewed as a place of authority, law, and bureaucracy. This is telling the civil servants and populace of the nation to do their jobs honourably and without bias; for the rule of law to be upheld. Many of the courts are between the 3 rivers that join around Važrac, which are prone to flooding occasionally. To be swept into the rivers, one has to leave the courts: this is a parable for abandoning the rule of law, which must be abided by.

- And Perenxše Declared: Those who seek the libraries of Panāc, remember the walls envelop you; not your mind nor sense of reason.
	- Panāc was chosen as one of the main archives of the empire due to its isolation from wars in the north, and its proximity to [[Ancurāna]] for scholarly co-operation. The walls of the libraries encompass you, your ability to reason, *and* the knowledge within: this is a way of saying people ought to take in knowledge when they are able to, and not wall off new ideas. In other words, people should critically think and not rely solely on internal biases.

# Cultural Influence
Perenxše Hasya came to define Xšaŧa, and its moral codes guided many aspects of law. More societal nuances, like gender and marriage, are not explicitly mentioned by Asxiŷr and left for cultural connotations to guide. Movement for more flexible roles in society came about from the fact the morals encouraged forward thinking and deliberation, rather than denouncement of such ideas as sinful.

When Acašyu supplanted Xšaŧa, a major reason for the quick adoption and synergy of Xšaŧan customs into Aciš ones was that one of the Songs explicitly mentions riders from the plateau from along the Ālu (the route they took), accepting and helping those who were deemed sinful, and the idea that if they stayed in the lowlands that they would grow more to be like the Xšaŧans with time - effectively waiting them out. This sort of worked, as the cultures influenced one another, but the religion of the Xšaŧan people was one of the most immutable aspects of their culture - the Acašyu nobility converted only a few generations into ruling over the empire.

Perenxše Hasya remains influential throughout the sphere of Xšaŧan (and later Gixanê) influence, from Yebezecāl to Castarŧ. Its malleable teachings and philosophical, tenacious outlook led to it being described partially as a philosophy compared to a religion, in many aspects. 

Perēni temples have their doors to the north, symbolizing that as you enter you are facing south: a rejection of Qiriýtaq and Anselesna. Temples are generally austere and hexagonal, for the Six Songs. 

Temple hierarchy is fairly uncontrolled: as a religion bordering on philosophy, there is little oversight, but each of the ancient cities in Gixanu has its own temple complex whose territory includes surrounding lands. These are then subject to control from the Conclave of Perenxše. The Emperor is expected to support and espouse the temples and the Perēni morals, as the emperors did when Asxiŷr led the nation in the Nightborn Era. 

In Êîlur, Salenuž, and the Perēni contingent in Castarŧ: the temple hierarchy is akin to the one in Gixanu, but the King of Êîlur (who nominally claims the title of Exiled Xšaŧan Emperor) is the figurehead. Perēni adherents around Lake Aciš in Halevnê, which used to be a majority region for the religion, have been persecuted into a small plurality by the government.
