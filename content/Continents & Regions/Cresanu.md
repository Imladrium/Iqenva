‍Cresanu is a region / subcontinent located on the continent of Našilu, stretching from the Starside to the tropics. There are a wide variety of terrains and climates, from the western Merŋulêan Mountains with their deep, ice–filled fjords, to the coastal arid deserts facing the Ausavčāl Ocean.

Cresanu is a term devised in [[Nulâašilu]] to describe all land north of the [[Halevnê|Halevnic Plateau]]; the denizens of Cresanu split their lands in a more comprehensive manner, seeing two further subcontinents. Merŋulêam in the west, comprising the frigid lowlands and Starside regions of Cresanu, and Brilnāxa in the east, describing the warmer portions of Cresanu to the east. Cresanu is also sometimes referred to as Ŋašilu Proper, in geological terms.

# Natural History and Resources
Cresanu was formerly joined with the continent of [[Anselesna]] to the north, for many millions of years, known as Hīryašna. Hīryašna and Nulâašilu collided to form a temporary supercontinent that quickly broke apart after only a few million years (410–380 MYA⊕) It was subsequently part of Saryašna, the most recent supercontinent 300–200 MYA⊕, which due to its position over the [[Sunside|Sunpole]] caused a mass extinction. It remained an integral part of Ŋašilu during the breakup of Saryašna, although still distinguished from Nulâašilu due to the Halevnic Plateau, severely weathered but still a formidable obstacle.

The subcontinent is moderately rich in terms of resources, with the greatest concentration of metallic deposits in the [[Velun]] Mountains in the east of the region. Other reserves can be found in the Merŋulêan Mountains to the west, and the various flood basalt regions in the north and centre of the subcontinent, due to the rifting that tried and failed to break apart Cresanu many times throughout its history. Expansive cratons in the interior remain rich with more industrial metals, though they are not often used at the level of development in several areas. Peat deposits are common in the muskegs of the west, and coal in the east. Salt flats in the southern arid regions provide for a substantial trade of halites.
