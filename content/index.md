# The land of sun and stars

Tidally locked in a peculiar way, [[Iqenva]] has two halves; one shrouded in darkness, one eternally under the orange rays of its star [[Cētu]]. The [[Sunside]], and the [[Starside]]. Yet, the Cētu does not stay in the same spot of the sky over the course of the short year. Rather, it forms an ellipse as the planet gyrates, traced over the tropics of the planet; due to orbital eccentricity and a slight axial tilt.

Between these dominions of eternal night and day, a bizarre consequence of these orbital phenomena profoundly impacts the temperate regions. For thousands of kilometres, a near day–night cycle emerges, where the sun will move around in the sky in that same ellipse. Some lands on the fringes of the tropics end up with with long, mild days and brief nights, and other lands farther towards the Starside conversely have long, chilly nights and short, blazing days.

# The land of three peoples

The most numerous sentient species on Iqenva are the [[Reithi]], akin to both Humans and avians. While humanoid in almost all ways, Reithi have unusually strong arms which bear the capability, though not the function, of flight on their low–gravity, thick–atmosphere world. Consequently, they often take to the skies with winged constructs: for sport, transport, and warfare. They dwell throughout Iqenva—Sunside and Starside—barring only the highest plateaus and the seas, though their tactful and rigorous studies of various magical disciplines have allowed them to overcome environmental barriers once thought impossible.

The [[Velsari]] are akin to Reithi, but distinctly aquatic; clearly not a natural species, they have piscine tails in lieu of legs below their torsos. Far fewer in number and less technologically developed, The disparate Velsari have in some instances overcome their strenuous circumstances to establish powerful, maritime confederations at equal footing to their Reithi counterparts. In conjunction with the more commonplace magical studies of the world, the Velsari have made up for their technological lacking with an innate magic far less documented than those of their terrestrial counterparts. Indeed, this branch may be considered the 'last true magic' of Iqenva, with all else being treated merely as an extension of science and academics.

Lastly are the [[Felari]]. Both bold and reclusive, haughty and isolated, dwelling in some of the most remote mountains of the world, these sentient avians have a deep distrust other peoples. Commonly called Low Felari, their kin the High Felari act as demigods, many ruling and warring by proxy over Iqenva. Encounters with the Felari (both High and Low) and rare, but spawn tales of fear and death to those who are allowed to escape. It is sensed that they too possess their own versions of secretive arts and perhaps are the creators of all disciplines of magic, the nature of which is much–discussed among the academics of the Reithi.

> ## Inspiration
> This has been a project of mine for years, with two distinct iterations, and is my third major worldbuilding project. 
> 
> My first proper worldbuilding project, Yruil, featured a species called the Syranui, a technologically–advanced pterosaur–like species that had established an interstellar nation off of the back of hyperlanes connecting the stars. I always liked the idea of this, but I found the idea increasingly untenable to construct, and my knowledge of hard worldbuilding was scarce enough that I knew I would one day dislike it.
> 
> Fast forward, and my second project was a world called Ascelua. I focused upon one single continent, but I ultimately made the setting so Earth–like that I grew bored of it, although it did involve some concepts for magic that I still like the idea of today; however necessitating better execution.
> 
> I radically wanted to diverge from Earth for my third project; my first was too radically different, and my second was too similar. Hence I went with a world of people close to humans, but on a world that differed violently from Earth in many ways. I wanted two things: flight as a common method of transport, and tidal locking. I designed the atmosphere, planet size, and species to all incentivize the creation of wingsuit–like contraptions, and I made the star dim enough (and Iqenva close enough) for tidal locking. Everything else fell into place: I made a mockup of plate tectonics and climates based on what I researched about different atmospheric circulations by rotational rates, etc.
> 
> I constructed nations and magic systems. However, I was never satisfied that the world map itself was extremely flawed: it looked fine as a map, but distorted horrifically as a globe. I never liked the tectonics or the physical world, but I liked the countries and the stories. Hence, I redesigned the whole map.
> 
> I did far more research this time, and it helped that I was (and am) taking Earth science courses in university. I made a whole tectonic history that, while not perfect, was good enough for me to be satisfied. I remade the maps, topographic and political, and used it as a template on which to remake many of the same countries I designed the first time.
> 
> This is still a work in progress, and I am focusing mostly on the subcontinent of Nulâašilu. I made some of the lore involve the Syranui once again, and their backstory now integrates not only with Iqenva but allows me to create multiple worlds, all with the possibility of interacting with one another as time goes on, with a common theme / origin for any and all magic.
> 
> ## Credits
> I have done lots of research with different sources, and I've been inspired by several fantasy stories:
> 
> - JRR Tolkien: My first fantasy obsession was the Lord of the Rings. There are a few themes from Tolkien in my worldbuilding, but not nearly as distinct as the other sources. The Reithi are meant to be somewhat Elven in grace, though slightly avian also. Certain cities and regions are meant to be ethereal and one or two even sacrosanct, again following the vein of Tolkien's Elven settlements. Then again, who in recent times has worldbuilt and not been even slightly affected by Tolkien?
>
> - Brandon Sanderson: This is probably the most obvious inspiration. A world in a larger universe with the opportunity for more planets, all sharing a common theme for how different–yet–related magic systems developed, is intrinsically Sandersonian in worldbuilding. However, some design choices I made before reading a single one of his books, notably on the world of Ascelua. Others I deliberately tried to differentiate from Sanderson's Cosmere; he focuses upon the magic, plot, and characters, I want to focus upon the world. That said, several of his themes still made it through, in one form or another. I enjoy reading his books, and having someone come up with so many original ideas makes it hard to seem original on your own.
>   
> - Artifexian: A YouTuber with a penchant for being one of the foremost worldbuilding channels on the platform, Artifexian served as a direct inspiration for all of my hard worldbuilding. Determining what ideas for the motion of planets and moons are possible, day–night cycles, plate tectonics, geology, all of it, I used his research to sculpt my worlds, and to learn that worlds can be interesting without some magical aspect. The universe in and of itself is interesting, and his channel inspired me beyond mere worldbuilding.
>
> - Worldbuilding Pasta: An online blogger, he closely collaborates with Artifexian. Worldbuilding Pasta came to my attention after Artifexian, and his blog served as an even more in–depth reference for geology, terrain, tectonics, and calculating whether what I wanted for Iqenva was physically possible. He linked scientific papers that I could delve into, to ascertain whether something was realistic or not. For the second iteration of Iqenva, I heavily relied upon Pasta's blog, as it gave great detail for the construction of tidally locked planets.
>
> - Lastly, my best friend Max. Max was the one that introduced me to Artifexian, and by extension Worldbuilding Pasta. He showed me Brandon Sanderson. Without those, many of my projects would have never happened. During late night chats we would postulate and send ideas back and forth, making and refining maps, diagrams, art, anything and everything related to building our settings. With someone to share my world with during its construction, it kept things interesting. Max, thank you for being such a great friend! :)
