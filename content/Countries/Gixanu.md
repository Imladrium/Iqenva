Gixanu, officially the Gixanê State “Gixanêŧāru” is the largest country in Nulàašilu (spanning into Cresanu), one of the most populous, and also one of the colonizing states of the region. Largely isolated from the rest of Nulàašilu Proper, Gixanu is being eclipsed technologically as it struggles to modernize in the face of the Great Industrialization and hold off nomadic attacks from the west and north. That said, it still remains a powerful force to be reckoned with, and at one point controlled two Literamantic Catenas at once (Xţām and Ifašyan).

The region of Gixanu has been host to many sequential empires, all controlling roughly the same contiguous sphere of influence along the eastern shores of Ŋašilu. 

# Etymology
  
“Gixanê" is the demonym for the largest ethnic group of the country, dwelling from the city of Tīru to the sea, then north to the Ālu River. The Gixanê are the result of the mixing of several cultures, of which two dominated - the sedentary Xšaŧans inhabiting the Xšaŧâyu region first, and the nomadic Aciš from the highlands, who conquered them and imported many of their customs. The origin of the term "Gixanê" is debated, but some scholars believe it to be an exonym from the Xšaŧans meaning "dual blooded."

# History

## Settling and Učlêláxru

The first settlers from Cresanu entered the region of Gixanu and Xšaŧây around 10500 AR, forming a loose collection of city states and nomads. These settlers would gradually come to speak similar languages to one another, influenced through trading. 4758 AR saw the foundation of Učlêláxru, a former city state based in Važrac. Only a few hundred years after though, the Catana of Xţām was discovered and the capital relocated to the north. Učlêláxru is also known as the 1st Xšaŧa Empire.

Učlêláxru's influence as one of the only centralized states in the ancient, pre–Ancu history gave it enormous advantages over its weaker neighbours, as did the very nascently–understood Catena. Učlêláxru expanded its frontiers past the Hinuvêtan Mountains, into modern Êîlur, Salenuž, and Telcensā. However, these initial expansions were cut short: the Eruption of Nihar Abrāžya in 2824 AR in Anselesna affected every civilization on Iqenva with decreased harvests and light for decades, inducing planet–spanning famines. While the ash did not make it to Učlêláxru, the implications did, and the kingdom fractured in wars back into a series of city states raiding one another.

## The Xšaŧa Empire
By ~2200 AR, the eruption was but an old tale, and new polities started to form not only in modern Gixanu, but elsewhere. One of these was a formerly nomadic group that had taken over Xšaŧa, the region about the Xšaŧây River within Gixanu. Originally based in Tīru before relocating the capital to Xţām in 2067 AR like Učlêláxru before them, the 2nd Xšaŧa Empire is what most people think of when "Xšaŧa" is mentioned. Colloquially, "Xšaŧa" is still a term used for Gixanu in some archaic writings.

Initially continuing as a hereditary monarchy like Učlêláxru, Xšaŧa suffered a coup by the Literamancists dubbed as the Caster Coup in 1216 AR. The Literamancists created a system of government ruled by a council of Literamancists, and they sharply controlled the supply and distribution of glyphs to maintain their power. Advancing in Xšaŧan society was often tied to the affairs of the government, with the Literamancists only taking select apprentices on merit to elevate through society. This is the first instance of a "Literacracy" on Iqenva, though there would be others to follow.

The Literacratic system—while controlling and hierarchical—was stable, because the imbalance of power was sufficient to keep rebellions down. There was a hereditary line of emperors, but they largely had ceremonial and spiritual duties. Within the government of Xšaŧa there was near–continuous plotting, backroom deals, and subterfuge. Innovations in glyphmaking and efficiency were important edges, and since the elevation to become a Literamancist in Xšaŧan society was based on merit and not lineage, there formed a close analog to the noble houses of other countries—the guilds. 

Xšaŧa was long–dominated by a power balance between various Literamantic guilds, which slowly with time deviated from their original purpose of shared knowledge bases and allegiances to become political organizations, with different strongholds throughout the country. Xţām and Istāla (the region about Xţām and the Istāl river) was the centre of all these guilds, but the outer regions had various allegiances and strongholds. While united by a mostly common culture, military, and society—Xšaŧa internally was greatly fragmented between the influences of guilds. The number of guilds fluctuated over time, but generally there were 3 primary ones and a varying number of smaller guilds, tied and allied to the major 3.

The height of this internal squabbling is what led Ancurāna to attack in the 1st War of the River in 1100, where Xšaŧa was forced to cede Castarŧ and thereby the tolls along the Šanrāxu river. The war momentarily forced the 3 guilds to work together in repelling the Ancu. After the war, Xšaŧa would see a longer period of lesser internal strife, which along with good affairs in Ancurāna led to a general golden age for Nulâašilu between 654–454 AR. While the Golden Years end for Ancurāna in 454 due to an assassination of their emperor, Xšaŧa maintained (relative) stability for a while longer, only fighting skirmishes to their western and northern frontiers. In 370, disagreements among the guilds over attitudes towards the Felari increased tensions once again.

Some guilds start to grow under the sway of certain High Felari, especially Asxiýr who had taken an interest in the country. Asxiýr didn't want to intervene directly because of the Treatise of Cētu, but had to support Xšaŧa (and the rest of Ŋašilu) against Anselesna and Qiriýtaq.

Qiriýtaq's agents subverted the opposing guilds, and the two sides nearly erupted into civil war. Ancurāna backed the side of Asxiýr, and operative cells of Qiriýtaq would flee to the north of Xšaŧa. The same year in 206 AR, the 3 powers of Ŋašilu (Ancurāna, Xšaŧa, and Brilnaxa) all signed the Pact of Ŋašilu against Anselesna's ambitions.

The 1st War of Wings saw Ŋašilan forces try to aid Tes Gaţel, but ultimately the fall of [city] led to further Anselesnan advance into Cresanu. The 1st War of Wings saw the Fall and subsequent Liberation of Brilnaxa, during which there were attacks on the northern lands of Xšaŧa in Telcensā. 

The Ancu Interregnum saw Xšaŧa intervene, and try to support a candidate that would be more favourable and less hostile to Xšaŧan lands; at the time, vassals of Ancurāna would often raid Xšaŧan lands and the emperors turn a blind eye, thus Xšaŧa in turn tried to reclaim their ancient stronghold in Castarŧ, sparking the 2nd War of the River. When the Interregnum ended, Ancurāna promised that for the return of Castarŧ they would enforce a permanent status of inviolability for Xšaŧan lands by their vassals, and permanent alliance. With the governor imposed by Xšaŧa on Castarŧ being unpopular Xšaŧa decided to relent, and the Treatise of Setec (now known as Seŧeu, in Salenuž) formed the Nulâašilan Alliance.

The 2nd War of Wings was far longer, and consisted of several stages of intermittent warfare throughout northern and central Ŋašilu over a 539 Cycle (226 Earth Year) long period. In 529 Anselesnan influence successfully toppled the Diarchy in Brilnaxa, and installed two supporters of Qiriýtaq (this was known as the God's Coup). Xšaŧa saw this as a threat and called Ancurāna to war as per their alliance, but only pressure from Asxiýr saw Ancurāna honour that alliance. Telcensā was conquered by Brilnaxa, and for hundreds of years became a land of intermittent war. This culminated in the Fall of Telcensā and the Siege of Xţām, which became a turning point in the overall 2nd War of Wings. The many battles and flights over the bay east of Xšaŧa eventually gave it the name "The Bay of Wings" as a result.

This is when another High Felaru, Hyanéq, decided to intervene. She was the one that offered to turn the folk of southeastern Anselesna (Haqalu) into the first Velsari, where they migrated south to the shoals and banks of Ilyān. With Qiriýtaq once again establishing a presence nearby, Hyanéq decided to act against the will of many of the other High Felari and support Asxiýr in his defence of Nulâašilu. Ilyāni forces cut off the Anselesnan naval blockade of Tunčnehaz in eastern Xšaŧa, and this eventually resulted in peace over the Bay of Wings and the ability to help the besieged city of Xţām.

After the lifting of the sieges in Xšaŧa's heartlands, Qiriýtaq started to take a more defensive position. Telcensā as a land was hard to control, now with Ilyāni intervention starting to harry his navy and the mountains to the west making passage hard from Brilnaxa. As such he decided to abandon his gains in Telcensā and shore up the defences in Yebezecāl and Brilnaxa, two far more defensible locations. 

The Little Interlude thus began, and saw a period of relative peace across Ŋašilu. This ended with the War of Brothers in Ancurāna, where Qiriýtaq influenced the outcome of a succession that spurred strife between the island of Dānura and the outer provinces.

Bickering among the guilds had weakened central control over the army, timed by Qiriýtaq to avoid them interfering in the War of Brothers. Asxiýr tried to unite the guilds and stop the war, but the damage was done and by the time Xšaŧa could assist, Ancurāna was greatly decentralized. The Ancu Dissolution War and the Treaty of the 5 Lords dissolved Ancurāna once and for all in 1017 AV. 

Castarŧ and the eastern fringes of the empire were more loyal to the war effort than the 5 Realms proper, and as such a 6th Realm - Castarŧ - was also formed. It was not viewed officially by the other 5, and claimed by both Fiŷážca and Iŋatra. Xšaŧa supported Castarŧ, and viewed it as the remnant of the old Ancu government which was still loyal to the alliance. 

Xšaŧa played a diminished part in the remainder of the 2nd War of Wings, as Asxiýr went on to personally destabilize Anselesna's long-standing presence in Tes Gaţel, viewing the loyalty of Reithi as fickle after the Ancu Dissolution War.

Xšaŧa came to rely upon Asxiýr's stabilizing influence. With his absence and relocation to the north, the guilds once again started to scheme and put short term interests first, growing intrigue in the manors of Xţām and Važrac. Nomadic groups long left neglected and hemmed in towards the outer fringes of the empire, notably Telcensā and Aciš, grew bolder in their raids upon settled regions of the realm. By 123 AE, Xšaŧa has lost control of both regions but still claimed them.

One particularly ambitious warlord of Aciš, Nidrū of Elāţyāŧ, began a campaign that would take advantage of Xšaŧa's first weak moment since the Siege of Xţām. Secretly in communication with the Ancu Realms, he promised the cessation of Xšaŧan support of Azuan rebels and Castarŧ in exchange for covert military support. This act of betrayal by the Ancu Realms for these gains represents the final shattering of the Nulâašilan Alliance, and the bickering guilds this time had no High Felari pressure to unite as they once did to stave off defeat like in the 2nd War of Wings.

The Acašyu Empire supplanted Xšaŧa in 132 AE, the remnants of which fled to the Bay of Iŷnrah. These guildmembers (largely one guild that used the turmoil to purge their rivals) continued the old form of government, and claimed a continuation to the mantle of Xšaŧa; this was the beginnings of Êîlur as an independent country.

## The Acašyu Empire
Acašyu attempted to pursue a similarly aggressive foreign policy to all but the Ancu Realms, attempting to cross the Hinuvêtan Mountains to conquer Êîlur; however, the army of Nidrū was accustomed to flat terrain and not mountain passes, so any access to Êîlur was denied to them (the two access points at Castarŧ and Atam were naturally defensible, and served as fortresses for centuries). Castarŧ and Êîlur, each viewing the other as the rightful successor to their respective governments, allied in one of the longest lasting alliances of Nulâašilu, continuing to the present day. By some reckoning, this is considered an extension of the Nulâašilan Alliance. 

Instead, they focused on using the same system of government that Xšaŧa had in place for thousands of years to usurp the mantle of leadership, in a somewhat successful transition. The main differences involved a consolidation of power upon the imposed monarchy, with the guilds supplanted by families loyal to Nidrū. Literamancy, meanwhile, was divested from the guilds and instead utilized more by the public. Many of the Aciš invaders wanted to co-opt the power, but Nidrū saw that he could paint the guilds as tyrants - and himself as a liberator, not a barbarian - if he loosened restrictions upon the catenic arts. Thus, despite a nomadic invasion of a sedentary society, the average Xšaŧan was somewhat better off under Acašyan rule.

Acašyu ruled out of the city of Léž immediately, but formally moved the capital to Léž in 208 AE, for several reasons: to avoid the capital being implicated by catenic withdrawal, to symbolize the severance between the government and literacratic rule, and to move the capital closer to the loyal nomadic ethnicities from which the new government arose. Léž was also a prime port city, and the artery of trade for ocean-going ships with cargo bound for Xţām.

The new rulers, despite the many concessions, still struggled to be seen as anything other than new tyrants among the southern regions of the empire: variously known as Xšaŧâyu (Xšaŧay Land in Ancu, referring to the river), Xšaŧa Proper, or Ŋeša (The South). Here, the presence of the guilds was somewhat weaker compared to landowners of the fertile floodplains, who already extracted concessions for the sake of farms. Xšaŧâyu was the breadbasket for the entire empire and further region, and the landowners saw the freedom of literamancy as a destabilizing factor for their stranglehold over the region. Thus, Acašyu had to quell several noble revolts loyal to the old government, which were aided by Êîlur safely behind the mountains between 306-442 AE.

The open plains of Xšaŧâyu led for intermittent warfare between the serfdom-like landowners and the Acašyan government. Most battles were won by the Acašyans, but the southernmost province of Salenuž successfully defended lands southwest of the Xšaŧây River with Êîlan and Castarŧi backing. In the provinces north of the river, however, the landowners were routed and the system liberalized in the eyes of the serfs, somewhat.

Acašyu remained politically stagnant for the centuries to come, but culturally the integration of the Aciš nomads into sedentary Xšaŧan society profoundly affected the culture and demographics of the region. While Xšaŧâyu remained solidly Xšaŧan in culture, the intermingling in the north and central regions of Xšaŧans and Aciš led to the Gixanê, a blended culture that came to dominate the area around and north of Xţām. Xţām itself remained Xšaŧan due to not many of the Aciš being willing to tether themselves to the city, but the Gixanê came to be a plurality as far south as Iqāneš on the Xšaŧay Delta itself.

Ironically, the region of Aciš itself would soon be lost. Various Halevnic hosts began to skirmish with Acašyan sentries on the western side of Lake Aciš, before launching assaults on Acišām around 1018 AE. Its army caught in complacency, the city soon fell - but the subsequent Siege of Elāţyāŧ was won, and the northern regions retained. During this time, Êîlur and Salenuž conspired with the remaining Xšaŧan nobles in the southern lowlands (Ŋeša) for a revolt in the time of Acašyu's weakness. With Acašyu distracted by the Halevnic invasion, they successfully restore Xšaŧan independence as far north as Enaŧām. 

The Siege of Xţām failed to capture the city, and they were driven back by the Acašyans to the dividing range between Ŋeša and the central regions. To assert their rule over the city, Acašyu reinstated Xţām as the capital of the empire, tying themselves more closely with the old Xšaŧan Empire to try and discredit the new polity. Êîlur and Salenuž both dissolved and join Xšaŧa as autonomous provinces, then moved the capital to Važrac - the largest city of the region.

## The Dual Power Era

Between 1053-1550 AE, the modern country of Gixanu was split between Acašyu to the north and Xšaŧa to the south, the latter polity known as the 3rd Xšaŧa Empire. Both claimed Xţām as their nominal capital along with the associated catena: Acašyu controlled it but operated most government affairs out of Léž, while Xšaŧa consolidated power in Važrac. The two sides would skirmish often in the Bay of Wings and in the broad mountain passes between their adjacent capitals, but each was strong enough such that neither wished for outright war.

Acašyu had the catena and a superior military, while Xšaŧa had the primary agricultural region, better diplomatic relations, and a stronger economy. The border fluctuated back and forth in the border regions, but generally the peace was kept in a cold war between the rivals. 

Acašyu at the time remained an elected monarchy as was Aciš custom, while Xšaŧa was a partial literacracy; similar but more reformed from that of the 2nd Xšaŧa Empire, because of Êîlur and Salenuž retaining that style of government as rump states from the older polity.

Xšaŧa, due to its ties with Castarŧ and the use of the city state as a proxy against the Ancu Realms, faced the threat of the Ancu Realms to the southwest. Xšaŧa also - as Êîlur before it - impeded what otherwise would have been Ancu dominance of trade in the area. Therefore the Ancu Realms generally sided with Acašyu, as they did when Nidrū first invaded in 130 AE. However, Xšaŧa did have the support of Brilnaxa to the northwest of Acašyu, because of their common enemy. Brilnaxa long contested northern Telcensā for its access to the ocean, but control of the region from beyond the Cōar Mountains proved difficult.

Ultimately, it was the Ilyāni that tilted the balance. The Xšaŧans had a tendency to ignore Ilyāni tolls when shipping their goods, and tried to brute force their way out to the sea from ports on the Bay of Iŷnrah to get around the Tunčne Peninsula. The Ilyāni started attacking Xšaŧan shipments in 1530 AE, which meant that trade between the two halves of Xšaŧa - north and south of the Hinuvêtan Mountains - was severely disrupted, requiring more expensive overland travel. This also diverted naval resources away from Acašyu.

In 1541, Acašyu invaded and used the chaos to quickly subjugate the major cities of Xšaŧa. Êîlur and Salenuž once again broke away as they saw the region of Ŋeša fall, and this time Acašyu did not advance further than the provinces, instead offering peace - they knew that resistance in the southernmost provinces would not be worth the cost, and thus did not bother. To better claim their required territories, Acašyu in 1550 was formally renamed to Gixanêŧāru - The Gixanê State, or shortened to Gixanu.

## Gixanu
The term "Gixanê" in that time had shifted away from a symbol of nomadic assimilation, to one of compromise. Most of the population around the Xšaŧây Delta at the time, the most populous part of Ŋeša, had been influenced by Aciš customs even in spite of Xšaŧan rule; even Xţām itself underwent cultural shift, in spite of the city being insular due to the catena's binding of the local residents. It was now seen as more prestigious to be Gixanê than Xšaŧan, and the upper classes sought to emulate their ruler's customs. Hence, Xšaŧan was increasingly pushed to be a "backwater" language and culture, though it held strong in southern Ŋeša near the Êîlan and Salenuži borders.

Gixanu agreed to pay tolls to the Ilyāni, which helped them to throttle the shipping east of the Bay of Iŷnrah. They even took the long-held city of Atam from the Êîlans, with Ilyāni assistance. Êîlur and its allies saw that they had no choice, and paid increased tolls as compensation in 1568. Gixanu restricted their vessels to south of the Tunčnê Peninsula unilaterally, to protect and almost monopolize trade with eastern Cresanu.

With this economic victory, Gixanu was in a prime position to further expand its influence further north. Brilnaxa primarily relied upon the Pass of Yebezecāl for any and all ocean access, while Gixanu was monopolizing almost all Nulâašilu-bound trading vessels. Gixanu was able to pressure Yebezecāl into greater trade rights out of their ports, controlling far more of the trade between Cresanu and even Istarthu. The Ancu Realms all used Sastranivê as a bypass, and thus the two operated in parallel - both Gixanu and the Ancu Realms agreed to keep to their own spheres of influence, but this noticeably excluded multiple other countries.

Gixanu reached its peak at this time. However, a string of political scandals regarding corruption of various figures in government directed the state's attention further inwards, opening slight cracks for other nations to exploit. That along with a concerted effort by even Gixanê allies to gain better access overseas aligned everyone to the cause of breaking the monopoly. Brilnaxa began it by raiding and crippling the ports they used to control along the coasts of Yebezecāl, reducing access for Gixanu to reinforce before their main army came over the mountains. In 1955 the War of the Coasts had started, in a series of rapid victories Brilnaxa retook the ports.

This was the beginning of the end for the monopoly. The various countries in Istarthu were becoming more adept at navigating around the Sunstorm, and thus between the advancing technological capacity of Gixanu's rivals and the loss of trade supremacy, the country began to slip. 

Gixanu came to depend upon the revenue of tolls and trade that it no longer controlled, and while this did not prove catastrophic, it did gradually weaken the country over decades. Skirmishes with Salenuž over contested borderlands - which often resulted in stalemates - forced the military to adapt, and it remained moderately potent at holding the frontiers to the southwest - and Telcensā to the north.

The only true benefit that this loss brought was the renewal of strained ties with the Ancu Realms - which in spite of the spheres of influence trading, Iŋatra's rulers in particular began to eye Gixanu's monopoly with envy. Now that the monopoly was broken, this subsided.

Today, Gixanu remains a solid power in its sphere of influence, and a staunch ally of the Ancu Realms. While not as dominant as it once was, it is undergoing a difficult transition in the Glyphborn Era when it comes to adopting modern technology. Gixanu's airships are not as advanced as those of their rival Êîlur, nor its railways as extensive as Haxīr's, but the Eldest Empire of Ŋašilu has always done one thing better than its rivals: endure.

# Government
Gixanu operates today as a hereditary monarchy as was the case of Acašyu, with significant power delegated to a legislature that somewhat mirrors the ancient guilds of Xšaŧa. The emperor presides over the legislature, but can be removed with a 2/3 vote following the line of succession. 

The members of the legislature inherit their seats in different ways: some are hereditary (a holdover from Acašyu), some are elected (most often in the largest cities), and some in the Xšaŧan areas of Gixanu continue to elect their representatives with the guild method. The Xšaŧans tend to be the most socially conservative, while the democratically elected urban representatives tend to be the most progressive. 

Most government offices are based out of Léž to prevent Catenic Withdrawal for members of government, but the official capital remains Xţām. By all *de facto* accounts, Léž is considered a second capital of the country. The emperor has two residences, one in each city. 

# Geography & Climate
Most of Gixanu is in the Orangebelt, with some fringes in the Redbelt. No part of the country experiences twilight, though the southwesternmost fringes have Cētu reach within 3° of the horizon at minimum. Gixanu consists of 3 main regions: Ŋeša (archaically known as Xšaŧâyu), Anistāl, and Telcensā. 

## Ŋeša
Ŋeša is considered to be the region around the Xšaŧây River in the south of the country - forming a verdant, flat valley between the Hinuvêtan and Epamārî Mountains. It is the most populous and most industrialized region of the country, but also is the least defensible. (Ŋeša means "\[The] South" in Old Acašyan, a name that stuck after the Fall of Xšaŧa.) It is the wettest region, with a warm climate. Rainfall is highest on the windward slopes of the Epamārî Mountains, which feeds the many tributaries of the Xšaŧây River. Some rivers also flow from the Hinuvêtan Mountains to the south, though these are less influential. 

To the northeast of traditional Ŋeša is Tunčne, a slightly more arid land shielded from the same level of moisture by the mountains comprising the peninsula and their angle relative to the southwesterly winds. It is a hilly, windy, hot promontory splitting the Bay of Wings from the Bay of Iŷnrah, where the Hinuvêtan Mountains jut out into the ocean. While viewed as a smaller region in its own right, it is often considered culturally part of Ŋeša. The southeastern side (closer to Êîlur) is more humid.

## Anistāl
Anistāl is the middlemost region: the area from the hills south of Xţām and the Istāl River, north to the Ālu River. The Ālu is the traditional boundary to Cresanu. Anistāl is less humid than Ŋeša, and hotter - both due to its position closer to the Sunpole, but also due to leeward winds from the Halevnic Plateau warming the plains east of the mountains. Roads notably go through and around Xţām, giving travellers the option to take faster routes via the region around Xţām affected by Catenic Withdrawal, or slower ones that avoid it. 

Anistāl also is sometimes expanded to the traditional area of Northern Aciš centred around the city of Elāţyāŧ, which is considered part of the Halevnic Plateau but remains under Gixanu control. Southern Aciš was lost to Halevnic hosts centuries previous, and the exact border between the two sundered regions remains fluid and tenuous.

## Telcensā
The northernmost, largest, and only Cresanyi region of Gixanu is Telcensā, which stretches from the Ālu River far to the Yebezecāl Mountains in the north, along the eastern coast of the continent. It is the poorest and least populous region, prone to nomadic incursions from over the mountains. It is also the hottest region, as it is closest to the Sunpole (barring the farthest portion of Tunčnê) with dry equatorial winds from the interior of Cresanu blowing over the mountains down to the narrow coastal plain.

# Religion and Culture

For religion, see the [[Perenxše Hasya]] page.

Culture of modern Gixanu is influenced by old Xšaŧan, and Aciš customs. The two synergized over centuries, but with other influences also: Ancu in multicultural areas by the southern coast, Azuan in the southwest, and Brilnaxan & Yebezecāli in the north of Telcensā.

In Ŋeša, a plant and grain-heavy diet dominates, due to all of the farms present. The opposite is true in Telcensā, where poorer ground favoured pastoral lifestyles and some semi-nomadic populations for meat-heavy diets. Anistāl represents a mixture of both. The import of foreign crops and food from Gixanu's time as a monopolistic trader across eastern Cresanu greatly enriched the diversity of food in the empire, and eating habits throughout. Using bread and skewers as cutlery are common in the south and central regions, while the more meat-heavy areas include knives with the skewers.

Yellow is seen as the imperial colour, because of ancient connotations tracing back to a personal preference of one of the first Xšaŧan emperors.

In ancient Xšaŧa, the role of community and family was very important due to both the Wars of Wings and the land system in place through much of the empire. This lessened during the time of Acašyu and later on industrialization, but nevertheless there is a sense of honour for one's relatives - at least those who are good members of their community. Social ostracising for greed at the expense of relatives, for example, tended to be common. 

Xšaŧa always placed heavy emphasis on music as a cultural tradition and pillar, to the point of some leitmotifs and chords representing words and ideas in the Xšaŧan language. This is most notable in religion, but it is common for music to integrate motifs and ideas, that can be understood effectively as a second set of lyrics or a formal, poetic method of speech.

Marriage was very important given the ties between families in communities, with complex ancestry-tracing. Marriage ceremonies often involve the formal announcement of union with musical language, not verbal language. Homosexual relationships are seen as slightly odd, but more accepted than not.
