Azušuŧ, officially “The United Confederation of Azušuŧ and Durušuŧ” (“Êr Utŋalu i Azušuŧ mev Durušuŧ”) is a large, decentralized state in the centre of Nulâašilu, with a very unique system of governance and a legalistic—yet martial—culture. Azušuŧ is an important power in Nulâašilan affairs, but has frosty relations with many of its immediate neighbours.

Azušuŧ is a politically complex country, with feudal allegiances intertwined with modern constitutions and parliament systems. Nominally with 3 capitals, with one of those currently under Ancu rule, Azuan intrigue has resulted in a slow centralization into to a duopoly of political powers.

# Etymology
“Azušuŧ” means “Eastbank” in Old Azuan, whilst “Durušuŧ” means “Westbank”; these are referring to the Ţānu River that flows through the state bisecting it. “Azušuŧ” has over time come to refer to the entire area abroad, while "Lehinišuŧ" is the geographically correct term: meaning "The Two Banks", but is rarely used. "Azua" has become a shortened version of "Azušuŧ", commonly used in conversation.

# History

## Old Azua (Sunborn Era)
The origins of the Azuan tribes has long been debated, although many have come to the conclusion that the Azuans as a people come from the merger of several disparate peoples. Some migrated from the east (kindred to the Gixanê), the original inhabitants of the area (who are related to the Invirans living in Cāduča), a splinter group of the nomads from Halevnê who crossed the Hinuvêtan Mountains, and the Ancu. Whatever the case, the early Azuans battled fiercely over land disputes stemming from this multitude of migrations. The Šanrāxu and Ţānu river valleys are very fertile, replenished from washed sediment down from the mountains.

The first sedentary settlements formed approximately in 1900 AR, which soon fell under the neighbouring influence of the Xsaŧans. The status quo of these loosely aligned cities paying tribute to Xšaŧa remained for several hundred years, until the Iŋiŷni migrated into lands to the west (~1400 AR). In Invires (modern northern Cāduča) and Durušuŧ there came to be an overlap of Iŋiŷni and the original Azuans. 

The Settling of Cāduča by Ancurāna then prompted Ancu influences to the south, and they were the first to try and assume direct control over Azuan affairs. This prompted a series of minor scuffles between the Xšaŧans and the Ancu, plus some loyal despots of Iŋiŷni and Old Azuan origin. The 1st War of the River in 1100 AR saw eastern Azušuŧ become a secondary theatre, which saw the Šanrāxu become more of a border than it had previously between Xšaŧan and Ancu influence in the region. 

The Northern Wars would come to change this; a loose alliance of Azuan leaders (supported by Xšaŧa) made use of a dynastic claim to one of the southern cities to repel the Ancu and their increasing colonization of the area. In 705 war broke out, and Ancu influence was diminished somewhat.

Ancu efforts would expand more to Durušuŧ in the west, which later became part of northern Cāduča. A series of lords loyal to Ancurāna in some Azuan cities, many of whom were heavy handed with 'justice,' prompted popular anger against Ancu influence once more. On a tour in 454 AR, the Ancu Emperor at the time was stabbed in an assassination, in the city of U'Mariléa (or as it was then known, Marēlye). Azuan and Duruan cities under Ancu influence faced retribution over this.

The status quo of Ancu and Xšaŧan influence partitioning Durušuŧ and Azušuŧ would not last however, as with the rise of Felari interference in politics of the two empires their focus drifted northwards to Anselesna. In 40 AR, the Northern Revolt resulted in a lesser degree of tribute to Ráemar, and the partitioning of some land from the governates to the south unto Azuan and Duruan rule. Xšaŧa, despite their lighter touch in Azua, decided to pre-emptively lessen tribute also in order to prevent a revolt when their gaze was directed by Asxiýr's influence to the north.

## Middle Azua (Nightborn Era)
This lessening of tribute into the Nightborn Era would have profound implications. The further diversion of interests of both empires from Azua, as well as the power vacuum that ensued. All of the various cities, guilds, nobles, etc. vied for influence in their respective polities. Azua fractured into a complex arrangement of feudal allegiances. This status quo would last most of the Nightborn Era. 

Neither Asxiýr nor Qiriýtaq saw much potential in Azua (at first), leaving them relatively free for a Ŋašilan people during the Wars of Wings. Disunified and quarrelling, it would have been harder to consolidate a proper government to control than to simply use Ancurāna and Gixanu as Asxiýr did. Border quarrels led to a loose alliance between the various patchwork of polities, with some again falling under Ancu and Xšaŧan sway. Most remained independent.

This gradual reassertion over outer Azuan lands led to the formation of the Azuan Union in 302 AV, which was less of a state and more of a loose coordination between states; a rivalries and skirmishes continued within Azuan polities, but external threats were presented a united front. Some polities were forcibly added to the union by others via coups and assassinations that precipitated and stained the first true Azuan unification. While there was a nominal monarch of the union, they were often ceremonial and only presided over meetings.

The Azuan Union would not face as dire threats as the loose principalities and polities did before but Ancurāna and Xšaŧa still vied for control over the larger member states when their attention wasn't forcibly diverted by Asxiýr's interventions. Azua reclaimed some borderlands from Ancurāna during the Ancu Interregnum around 351-428 AV.

With this increase in unity, in 470 AV Qiriýtaq did attempt to reignite old grudges in the nobility against the Ancu and Xšaŧans, which led to a few states being willing to destabilize the border regions of both empires on his behalf in exchange for land and supplies. However, Asxiýr personally travelled to Azua with a delegation of Ancu and Xšaŧan diplomats to delineate borders and foreign influence concerns. He also made some pointed threats to Azua if they allowed Qiriýtaq to continue meddling, and because Azua was so far and geographically isolated from the rest of Qiriýtaq's forces, they acceded.

The Azuan Union continued like this for most of the remainder of the Nightborn Era, however as Asxiýr grew more desperate he tried to take the reigns of the Azuan Union. Seeing the sorry state of affairs in Ancurāna and Xšaŧa, the Azuans made a stand against Asxiýr and assassinated their own pro-Nulâašilan Alliance king to replace him with his more Azuan-minded son (619 AV).

The War of Brothers in 913-920 in Ancurāna saw the last of the borderlands even ask for Azuan support, because of the uncertainty as Dānura tried to reign in the outer lands of the empire. The subsequent Invasion of Dānura in 1016-7 AV and fall of Xšaŧa in 132 AE led to the final nails in the coffin for foreign control over Azuan affairs. While disunified, benefitting from the misfortune brought at the hands of Asxiýr and Qiriýtaq's rivalry over Ŋašilu left a power vacuum that the locals were happy to take back.

## High Azua (AE)
UPDATE

While wars still occurred between the various duchies, kingdoms and city states, they were lessened to an extent. This policy of wars internally being allowed, but external threats creating a united front, started to shape a united Azuan identity. The two greatest Azuan states (Ēzta and Rĕvad) allied and set about unifying the region, politically and militarily. For centuries there was a period of relative peace following the chaos of the Nightborn Era. 

This peace was broken only by the Highlander Invasions of 1504, when Halevnic hosts started to raid the lowlands of Azušuŧ. With the raids making it as far south as the Ancu Realms, even Azuan rivals assisted them to repel the assault and shore up better defenses in the northern mountain passes.

After one last long war - finally, in 1805 AE, the Confederation of Azušuŧ was formed. Internal wars were disallowed, and the three capital became E’Đēŧasen in order to satisfy balance of power politics between Ēzta and Revad (whose capitals are Ānunāra and Iŷŧal, respectively). The religious nature of E’Đēŧasen reinforced it as a legitimate capital, far as it was from the periphery of the Azuan heartlands.

All the while, rival powers were seeking to keep the Azuans from unifying. Cāduča tried to politically isolate Ēzta and Revad, promising rival Azuan polities alliances (which never came to pass) and trade agreements, whilst embargoing Ēzta and Revad. Fiŷâzca managed to de facto annex long disputed land on the eastern frontiers of Azušuŧ, but this remained claimed by Azušuŧ. The status of southeastern Azušuŧ has alternated between the two powers multiple times since this first Fiŷzan conquest, and currently is under Fiŷzan control. That said, Salenuž, a long-time ally of Revad, supported Azuan unification, as did Rviŷah (the predecessor state of Astarviý).

The Azuan government’s complexity started around the unification too. While nominally an equal confederation of the Azuan states headed by a monarch, in reality it is a diarchy of Ēzta and Revad, where the monarchs of each state have great sway over the weak central government. The Azuan monarch is from a house created by the marriage of Ēzta’s and Revad’s royal lines, and is legally bound in many ways by Great Council (a body of representatives from the confederation members) which is effectively controlled by Ēzta and Revad. The two long-time allies have entered a political power struggle over many topics, uniting primarily in their shared desire to prove Azušuŧ’s status as a great power. Cāniše would spread from Azušuŧ, becoming predominant in the Salenuž, Êîlur, and parts of western Gixanu.

The conquest of Syentār in 1903 was the first major foreign war. The Azuans long coveted the mineral riches of the region, and justifying an excuse on religious matters came soon after unification. Syentār is currently referred to officially as the Eastern Crownlands, with some minimal local autonomy. Rivals of Azušuŧ often show Syentār as independent, but this is untrue and has been for centuries.

With the Great Industrialization starting in 2107, the Azuans have sought to capitalize on the change, their economic might (even in their decentralized state) eclipsing that of all nearby countries. Azušuŧ has made sure to maintain excellent relations with Salenuž, Êîlur, and Castarŧ to ensure access to the ocean for further trading abroad, as well as trade over the Hinuvêtan Mountains through Acišadah and to the Halevnic Basin. Âyacast remains a thorn in the side to them, but pressure from the Azuans has forced an allowance of trade nevertheless. Êîlur in particular acts as the gateway for the majority of imports to Azušuŧ from other continents, especially Êîlan colonies. 

In 2378, however, the Cādučans managed to win a decisive war, which resulted in the capture of E’Đēŧasen and the creation of the E’Đēŧasen Corridor. This enraged the Azuans, who view it as a sacrilege of their holy capital and all they’ve strived for. In the meantime, the capitals of Ēzta’s and Revad are the dual seats of power. As Cāduča wanes once more in strength, however, the Azuans plot for yet another war to reclaim their lost capital.
