Azušuŧ, officially “The United Confederation of Azušuŧ and Durušuŧ” (“Êr Utŋalu i Azušuŧ mev Durušuŧ”) is a large, decentralized state in the centre of Nulâašilu, with a very unique system of governance and a legalistic—yet martial—culture. Azušuŧ is an important power in Nulâašilan affairs, but has frosty relations with many of its immediate neighbours.

Azušuŧ is a politically complex country, with feudal allegiances intertwined with modern constitutions and parliament systems. Nominally with 3 capitals, with one of those currently under Ancu rule, Azuan intrigue has resulted in a slow centralization into to a duopoly of political powers.

# Etymology
“Azušuŧ” means “Eastbank” in Old Azuan, whilst “Durušuŧ” means “Westbank”; these are referring to the Ţānu River that flows through the state bisecting it. “Azušuŧ” has over time come to refer to the entire area abroad, while "Lehinišuŧ" is the geographically correct term: meaning "The Two Banks", but is rarely used. "Azua" has become a shortened version of "Azušuŧ", commonly used in conversation.

# History

## Old Azua (Sunborn Era)
The origins of the Azuan tribes has long been debated, although many have come to the conclusion that the Azuans as a people come from the merger of several disparate peoples. Some migrated from the east (kindred to the Gixanê), the original inhabitants of the area (who are related to the Invirans living in Cāduča), a splinter group of the nomads from Halevnê who crossed the Epamārî Mountains, and the Ancu. Whatever the case, the early Azuans battled fiercely over land disputes stemming from this multitude of migrations. The Šanrāxu and Ţānu river valleys are very fertile, replenished from washed sediment down from the mountains.

The first sedentary settlements formed approximately in 1900 AR, which soon fell under the neighbouring influence of the Xsaŧans. The status quo of these loosely aligned cities paying tribute to Xšaŧa remained for several hundred years, until the Iŋiŷni migrated into lands to the west (~1400 AR). In Invires (modern northern Cāduča) and Durušuŧ there came to be an overlap of Iŋiŷni and the original Azuans. 

The Settling of Cāduča by Ancurāna then prompted Ancu influences to the south, and they were the first to try and assume direct control over Azuan affairs. This prompted a series of minor scuffles between the Xšaŧans and the Ancu, plus some loyal despots of Iŋiŷni and Old Azuan origin. The 1st War of the River in 1100 AR saw eastern Azušuŧ become a secondary theatre, which saw the Šanrāxu become more of a border than it had previously between Xšaŧan and Ancu influence in the region. 

The Northern Wars would come to change this; a loose alliance of Azuan leaders (supported by Xšaŧa) made use of a dynastic claim to one of the southern cities to repel the Ancu and their increasing colonization of the area. In 705 war broke out, and Ancu influence was diminished somewhat.

Ancu efforts would expand more to Durušuŧ in the west, which later became part of northern Cāduča. A series of lords loyal to Ancurāna in some Azuan cities, many of whom were heavy handed with 'justice,' prompted popular anger against Ancu influence once more. On a tour in 454 AR, the Ancu Emperor at the time was stabbed in an assassination, in the city of U'Mariléa (or as it was then known, Marēlye). Azuan and Duruan cities under Ancu influence faced retribution over this.

The status quo of Ancu and Xšaŧan influence partitioning Durušuŧ and Azušuŧ would not last however, as with the rise of Felari interference in politics of the two empires their focus drifted northwards to Anselesna. In 40 AR, the Northern Revolt resulted in a lesser degree of tribute to Ráemar, and the partitioning of some land from the governates to the south unto Azuan and Duruan rule. Xšaŧa, despite their lighter touch in Azua, decided to pre-emptively lessen tribute also in order to prevent a revolt when their gaze was directed by Asxiýr's influence to the north.

## Middle Azua (Nightborn Era)
This lessening of tribute into the Nightborn Era would have profound implications. The further diversion of interests of both empires from Azua, as well as the power vacuum that ensued. All of the various cities, guilds, nobles, etc. vied for influence in their respective polities. Azua fractured into a complex arrangement of feudal allegiances. This status quo would last most of the Nightborn Era. 

Neither Asxiýr nor Qiriýtaq saw much potential in Azua (at first), leaving them relatively free for a Ŋašilan people during the Wars of Wings. Disunified and quarrelling, it would have been harder to consolidate a proper government to control than to simply use Ancurāna and Gixanu as Asxiýr did. Border quarrels led to a loose alliance between the various patchwork of polities, with some again falling under Ancu and Xšaŧan sway. Most remained independent.

This gradual reassertion over outer Azuan lands led to the formation of the Azuan Union in 302 AV, which was less of a state and more of a loose coordination between states; a rivalries and skirmishes continued within Azuan polities, but external threats were presented a united front. Some polities were forcibly added to the union by others via coups and assassinations that precipitated and stained the first true Azuan unification. While there was a nominal monarch of the union, they were often ceremonial and only presided over meetings.

The Azuan Union would not face as dire threats as the loose principalities and polities did before but Ancurāna and Xšaŧa still vied for control over the larger member states when their attention wasn't forcibly diverted by Asxiýr's interventions. Azua reclaimed some borderlands from Ancurāna during the Ancu Interregnum around 351-428 AV.

With this increase in unity, in 470 AV Qiriýtaq did attempt to reignite old grudges in the nobility against the Ancu and Xšaŧans, which led to a few states being willing to destabilize the border regions of both empires on his behalf in exchange for land and supplies. However, Asxiýr personally travelled to Azua with a delegation of Ancu and Xšaŧan diplomats to delineate borders and foreign influence concerns. He also made some pointed threats to Azua if they allowed Qiriýtaq to continue meddling, and because Azua was so far and geographically isolated from the rest of Qiriýtaq's forces, they acceded.

The Azuan Union continued like this for most of the remainder of the Nightborn Era, however as Asxiýr grew more desperate he tried to take the reigns of the Azuan Union. Seeing the sorry state of affairs in Ancurāna and Xšaŧa, the Azuans made a stand against Asxiýr and assassinated their own pro-Nulâašilan Alliance king to replace him with his more Azuan-minded son (619 AV).

The War of Brothers in 913-920 in Ancurāna saw the last of the borderlands even ask for Azuan support, because of the uncertainty as Dānura tried to reign in the outer lands of the empire. The subsequent Invasion of Dānura in 1016-7 AV and fall of Xšaŧa in 132 AE led to the final nails in the coffin for foreign control over Azuan affairs. While disunified, benefitting from the misfortune brought at the hands of Asxiýr and Qiriýtaq's rivalry over Ŋašilu left a power vacuum that the locals were happy to take back.

## High Azua (AE)
While wars still occurred between the various duchies, kingdoms and city states, they were lessened to an extent. The two greatest Azuan states (Ēzta and Revad) allied and set about unifying the region within the Azuan Union, politically and militarily. For centuries there was a period of relative peace following the chaos of the Nightborn Era. The Ancu states and the Azuans squabbled over the exact boundary of the border, but local resistance against Ancu incursions mostly curtailed these operations until the boundaries solidified.

The largest flash point was over E'Đēŧasen, fairly deep in Azuan lands, but holding a religious significance for the Ancu Realms. The native religion of the Azuans, Caniše, remained dominant but over time Sâiýme spread from E'Đēŧasen. Pilgrimages were allowed into the city, and it became a focal point of Ancu and Azuan cultural mixing.

Peace was broken only by the Highlander Invasions of 1504, when Halevnic hosts started to raid the lowlands of Azušuŧ. With the raids making it as far south as the Ancu Realms, even Azuan rivals assisted them to repel the assault and shore up better defenses in the northern mountain passes. The Ancu and Azuans went as far north as Salūah to shore up defences.

During the Dual Power Era of Gixanu, the Azuans cautiously supported their neighbours - the Xšaŧans - to try and stave off any potential conflict in the area. Different members of the Azuan Union supported each supported the two sides, but the pro-Xšaŧan side held more of the important states and duchies, including Revad. This continued as Xšaŧa fractured again, where the Azuans relied on Salenuž for access to the sea - though maritime shipments at that time were less important than the present.

All the while, rival powers were seeking to keep the Azuans from unifying any further. Cāduča tried to politically isolate Ēzta and Revad, promising rival Azuan polities alliances (which never came to pass) and trade agreements, whilst embargoing Ēzta and Revad. Fiŷážca managed to de facto annex long disputed land on the southeastern frontiers of Azua, but this remained claimed for a while after. The status of southeastern Azušuŧ has alternated between the two powers multiple times since this first Fiŷžan conquest, and finally remained under Fiŷžan control. That said, Salenuž, now an ally of the Azuans for a long time, supported Azuan unification, as did Rviŷah (the predecessor state of Astarviý). Finally, in 1805 AE, the Confederation of Azušuŧ was formed after a treatise was formed between the remaining dominant powers. Internal wars were disallowed, and the three capitals became E’Đēŧasen in order to satisfy balance of power politics between Ēzta and Revad (whose capitals are Ānunāra and Iŷŧal, respectively). The religious nature of E’Đēŧasen reinforced it as a legitimate capital, far as it was from the periphery of the Azuan heartlands. By this point Sâiýme has supplanted Caniše as the dominant religion, though it remained prolific in the highlands.

The conquest of Syentār in 1903 was the first major foreign war. Technically an Azuan principality from the days of the Azuan Union, Syentār slowly drifted away from the affairs of the Union to gain de facto independence, with the mountains between it and the Azuan heartlands. With its reintegration (much to the chagrin of the locals) Syentār is currently referred to officially as the Eastern Crownlands, with some minimal local autonomy. Syentār is also influenced by the Halevnic tribes to the north, some of whom settled in the humid valleys.

With the Great Industrialization starting in 2107, the Azuans have sought to capitalize on the change, their economic might eclipsing that of several nearby countries. Azušuŧ has made sure to maintain excellent relations with Salenuž, Êîlur, and Castarŧ to ensure access to the ocean for further trading abroad, as well as trade through Ahrnêl and Esaceš to the Iŋiŷnim nations. Fiŷážca remains a thorn in the side to them, but pressure from the Azuans has forced an allowance of trade nevertheless, even despite loss of their southeasternmost borders. Êîlur in particular acted as the gateway for the majority of imports to Azušuŧ from other continents, as Gixanê influence over the Ausavčāl Ocean waned.

In 2378, however, the Cādučans - recently run by an unstable, zealous ruler - managed to win a decisive war against the odds posed to him by his advisors, which resulted in the capture of E’Đēŧasen and some of the Ahrnêli lands to the west. E’Đēŧasen, being so close to the Ancu border and yet so important, was one of the most fortified places in Nulâašilu, but the defences lagged as a long peace meant the illegal redirection of much-needed money to more lavish purposes in the military. The capitals of Ēzta’s and Revad now remain as the dual seats of power, but many view Cāduča's victory as one based on luck rather than tactical skill. As Cāduča wanes once more in strength, however, the Azuans plot for yet another war to reclaim their lost capital.

# Government
The Azuan government’s complexity is infamous. While nominally an equal confederation of the Azuan states headed by a monarch, in reality it is a consociational diarchy of Ēzta and Revad, where the monarchs of each state have great sway over the fairly weak central government. The Azuan monarch is from a house created by the marriage of Ēzta’s and Revad’s royal lines, and is legally bound in many ways by the High Council (a body of representatives from the confederation members) which is effectively controlled by Ēzta and Revad. The two long-time allies have entered a political power struggle over many topics, primarily relating to economic differences between them resulting from the Great Industrialization. 

The legislature (High Council) has a specific number of seats assigned to Ēzta and Revad each, but the two assign those seats differently. Ēzta has a representative democracy under a constitutional monarchy, where the monarch and appointees by the monarch are given 20% of the High Council seats and the remaining 80% are elected. Revad meanwhile is more authoritative, with many of the seats coming from powerful familial lines and mercantile enclaves. 

Ānunāra is the legislative capital, while Iŷŧal is the executive capital. E'Đēŧasen used to be the auditory and judicial capital, but these tasks have been reassigned to the other two capitals after the Cêras War.

# Geography & Climate
While Azua is traditionally split east-west culturally, the two main geographic regions consist of the mountainous north (Salhyāra) the flatter south (Anhāna).

## Salhyāra
 The north abuts the Epamārî mountains, which are rich in various minerals. This area is extremely humid and faces consistent precipitation, forming a temperate rainforest as the elevation of the Halevnic Plateau forces rain to precipitate on the mountainsides. This rainforest spans the lower slopes of the Epamārî mountains, colder temperatures in the west and warmer in the east. Alpine conditions can be found higher up, with some of the few ice sheets found on the Sunside nestled in the highest mountains on the border between Azua and Halevnê. Many of the largest rivers of Nulâašilu all start in Salhyāra, including the Cêras, Edu, Ţānu, and Šanrāxu.
 
## Anhāna
The south is where the majority of the population resides, and where most of the arable land is. Anhāna is a lower highland area separated from the Ancu Plain by the Ancuryehel, a range of rolling hills that delineates the southern boundary of much of Azua. The plains are some of the best farmland in all of Nulâašilu, with the water taking minerals from the mountains and enriching the soils. The population of Azua is substantially higher than its fertile land area would suggest, with a developed countryside and urbanized cities. 

# Religion and Culture
Azua is mostly Sâiýme, having long been in the Ancu cultural sphere in terms of religion. They held the city of E'Đēŧasen for millennia, and the highly autonomous duchies and states of Azua meant there was little centralized authority to try and prevent its spread. The native religion of the Azuans, Cāniše, was relegated and pushed back into the mountainous vales near the Halevnê border, in Salhyāra. 

Azuan culture is influenced by Ancu and Salenuži norms, depending on region. The western half of the country (Durua) has more Ancu tendencies, such as Sâiýme being engrained in society more but still retaining their own language and customs. The east (Azua) has more Salenuži customs. Both are fairly industrious, and loyalty to local states and duchies has a tendency to sometimes overrule Azua as a country due to the long history of decentralization.

Cuisine tends to be fairly meat-averse in all regions of the country, except for poultry which remains an exception. This is a remnant of Cāniše, which saw eating most kinds of meat as sinful.  The northern mountains is heavily reliant on poultry and vegetarian foods, because they represent some of the last adherents of the old religion.
