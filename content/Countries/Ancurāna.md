Ancurāna, also known as the Ancu Realms, is a large and informal supranational confederation in the southeast of [[Nulâašilu]]. Formerly a country in its own right, Ancurāna is the progenitor of the so–called "Ancu Sphere", one of the main ethnic, religious, and political divisions of Nulâašilu.

Ancurāna is comprised of 5 equal "Realms": Cāduča, Fiŷážca, Iŋatra, Penaxra, and Mezāvca, all former administrative units of the empire, their borders with one another only minimally altered post–Ancu breakup. In addition to the 5 Realms, the subordinate "Condominium of [[Dānura]]" controls the eponymous Isle of Dānura on behalf of the 5 Realms, with a local government to handle some domestic affairs. The City State of [[Castarŧ]] to the northeast is sometimes considered as a 6th Ancu Realm, and used to have the same status before the Xudūši War. Velám is a condominium of Penaxra and [[Haxīr]] to the west, ruled in a similar manner to Dānura. Lastly, Iŋatra and Penaxra have overseas colonies, most notably the Dominion of Sastrānivê on the eponymous isles east of Nulâašilu.


# Etymology
"Ancurāna" means "Empire of Ţāna"; "Ţāna" is the dynasty that controlled the state for much of its existence. Ergo, the partly Anglicized term "Ancu Empire" literally translates to "Imperial Empire" which makes little sense, and is improper.

# History

## Formation of Ancurāna
Before the existence of Ancurāna, much of the lands about and in the empire's eventual domains were filled with various tribes and smaller kingdoms, with technology resembling our Late Antiquity. The Fážri Kingdom, which was based around modern-day country of Fiŷážca and is seen as its partial-predecessor, was the most powerful of these states, controlling much of the Blue Sea's coastline. In the city of Isim, at the time the capital of a kingdom of the same name, had recently lost a war against the Fážri Kingdom over the island of Dānura. Ráemar at the time wasn't founded, but nevertheless the island at the time was important to projecting authority over the Blue Sea, but yet unknown for the [[Catenas|Catena]]. The King of Isim, Halua, was a young, cunning, militaristic, and vengeful man, ever looking for justifications to project his own authority and power through conquests. He was skilled in warfare. 

Halua "The Daggerlord" e'Ţāna first rose to prominence as the 2nd son of the Queen, who noticed a hidden strength in the otherwise seemingly stoic boy. He was trained as a military tactician in the Fázri Kingdom's military academy, in their capital of Eŋān (near modern-day Âvun), and sent to the battlefield in wars in Isim's western frontier. After a few years and a successful expansion of their frontier further into the plains of the west, Halua would return to the city of Isim, and then conduct several other expansionistic wars. 

This rapid expansion had drawn the ire of the Fážri Kingdom, seeing Isim, and Halua himself, as a threat to Fážri interests. After the Queen of Isim refused to back down, the King of Fážri declared war. The Fážri navy and airforce quickly captured the Isimi island of Dānura, and had then besieged the city of Isim, annihilating Halua's army. Halua was cut off from the city of Isim, his parents, and brother. The city would be captured after a very lengthy siege, during which the soldiers lost their discipline (due to frustration of the length of the siege) and entered the royal palace, pillaged it, and killed the royal family. The Fážri general was appalled, and reigned in his own troops.

Halua, now King of Isim, was contacted by the Fážri general and informed of the state of affairs. Enraged at the death of his family, the ruination of his army, and the capture of his city, Halua was forced to sign peace forming buffer states between the two kingdoms, the ceding of the island of Dānura, and significant reparations. This was known as the Peace of Humiliation, and the young man would bear a hatred of the Fážri Kingdom for the rest of his life.

Halua would bide his time, rebuilding his army and Isim. He coordinated the League of Penaxra into alliance, and started moving agents into motion.

15 years after the Peace of Humiliation, he set his plan into motion, and sent assassins into the Fážri Kingdom; despite its strong and intimidating external presence, the kingdom was internally chaotic and fragmented. The inheritance was highly volatile at times. It so happened that the king at the time was somewhat infertile, and only managed to produce one child. The succession past the child was uncertain, with multiple claimants.

The assassins murdered the heir and the king on the same fateful night in the year 1734 AR, sending the Kingdom into a succession crisis. Fážri's enemies leapt on the opportunity, attacking the overextended and unstable kingdom during the civil war. Halua was unable to reconquer Dānura as of yet, because of the strength of the Fážri navy which had remained largely united. Halua grew only angrier at this, and decided to march into Fážri territory via modern-day Mezāvca, leaving part of his army behind to defend Isim against any naval invasions. After several years of bitter war between the Fážri Kingdom & her allies, the various enemies of Fážri, and internal enemies in the Fážri civil war, Halua stood before the gates of Eŋān as a member of the victorious coalition. Halua pressed for the harshest peace terms among the delegates, but his wrath only grew when the conference eventually granted the Fážri Kingdom clemency from multiple proposed territorial and economic concessions. Halua argued that his stance ought to have been followed, considering that without Isim it is unlikely that the Fážri kingdom would have been defeated. In the peace treaty, Isim regained some land from Fážri buffer states, but Dānura was split politically between Isim and Fážri, rather than the entire island being reannexed—much to his displeasure.

Feeling betrayed, Halua assessed his options. The coalition warned Halua to not violate the tenuous peace, and threatened to join Fážri against Halua should he invade. Halua needed to either intimidate or convince the coalition to invade once more, and to see the previous peace as a mistake. He chose intimidation. One of the greatest strengths of Halua was his proclivity towards intrigue. Halua had maintained ties with several mercenary guilds, assassins, and other shady individuals, using these connections as thinly-veiled threats to people who drew his ire. Halua did try this during the peace conference, but the coalition did not know much of his reputation at the time. Arranging for assassins to strike in the capitals of every coalition state who had opposed his demands, over the course of three years 21 officials all met mysterious ends. This purge earned him the epithet "The Daggerlord". He made sure to spread rumours about his assassins' reach and capability. The League of Penaxra, one by one, crumbled into the direct fold of Isim.

Halua then ordered an army to march via land around the Blue Sea, capturing the coast, whilst he marched from South Dānura. On the way, Halua was nowhere to be found, but when he returned people noticed a change about him. He became more confident, ruthless, less diplomatic, his appetite doubled. When news reached the former coalition members about the capture of North Dānura, and much of the Fážri coast, none dared oppose him. Seeing this, as well as the still-weakened state of Fážri after the brutal war, he sailed across the sea and marched to Eŋān, intent on pressing for further concessions. That night in camp outside of the city, one of his own assassins turned on him (he was paid by the Fážri government in secret). It is debated how Halua survived the ordeal, for he was stabbed directly in the heart with one of his own poisoned daggers. Most likely, Halua's alteration a few weeks prior was him gaining Innateglyph powers by unknown means, granting himself unnatural healing abilities. He killed the assassin with the bloody dagger, and from then on his paranoia grew wild. Immediately suspecting the Fážri government (as it happened, correctly), his wrath led him to, upon capturing the massive city, raze it to the ground in a massive escalation and as one of the worst atrocities of the time. 

By this point, he controlled roughly 60% of Fážri; yet he proclaimed the annexation of the entire Fážri Kingdom into Isim. Even the intimidated coalition couldn't ignore this, and declared war. The remaining Fážri forced and the coalition amassed and marched south from provisional Fážri capital of Ţîráy, winged scouts flying ahead of the main army, punching through the coast to Fiŷanulār to cut off his land supply route. Fortunately for Halua, this is exactly what he wanted them to do, because it left the Coalition's army also somewhat overextended. Sending word to an army in the west to distract the coalition army for the time being, and to hold Fiŷanulār at all costs, he rushed with all speed to Ţîráy, ravaging the countryside for supplies. There, he captured the city after threatening to raze it just like Eŋān if they did not surrender, scaring the leaders into agreeing. He marched back to Fiŷanulār, where his two armies crushed the coalition's in the decisive Battle of Fiŷanulār. With his venture to Ţîráy, there were no major Fážri cities left to capture, and much of the northern countryside folded to his control.

Halua would then march around the Blue Sea, conquering the various Coalition states over a couple years until he brought the entirety of the coast under his control. With his enemies defeated, Halua announced the creation of a new state: Ancurāna. The Ţāna Empire. He founded the city of Ráemar and moved his capital there in later years, only telling his most trusted inner circle about the truth: the Catena of Dānura.

## Later History
Halua became the first emperor of Ancurāna; much of his reign was spent quelling rebellions in his new empire, as well as instituting a new administration system, legal code, and internal reorganization. Halua lived for a very long time: he lived for 203 Earth years: over 2.5 times the average life expectancy at the time. Scholars almost universally agree that he had discovered (and kept secret) the Innateglyphs for his long lifespan and other strange events throughout his life. Many have attempted to find his secret, but none have done so. While the Catena became public knowledge towards the end of his reign, the deals he made with High Felari were not. In particular, Qirāsu.

The next emperor after Halua, his great-great-great-granddaughter Dalira (there are no gendered distinctions in many Ancu titles), would be the one to first notice the effects of inhabiting the island of Dānura, and becoming Islebound, upon trying to leave the island for a whaling hunt. "Ráemar" means "Miracle" to describe the unknown change that Halua underwent at this spot, all those years ago, but it became a cage for those who dwelt in the presence of power. Dalira was a calm and relatively peaceful leader, very much the opposite of Halua in that regard, and her reign solidified prosperity over the empire. She invested much within internal infrastructure, namely the standardization of wings, landing areas, road-building, etc. Under her reign, the surrounding land was dug out and the Palace of Ráemar was built around the Catena. 

The War of the River took place with the [[Gixanu|Xšaŧa Empire]] in 1100 AR, which yielded control of the strategic region of Castarŧ. Ancurāna attacked, noting that Xšaŧa at the time was highly fragmented internally due to rival guilds in its government. At this time, Ancurāna would stretch from the Šanrāxu River to the Inner Channels; from the Iŋiŷn River and the Eteryahal range to the Bay of Iŷnrah.

Ancurāna would often battle with the Azuans to the north, the Mablusans to the west, the Cêrni to the northwest, and against piracy from the [[Acatūn|Havens of Avi]]. The boundaries often would fluctuate, with every new war adjusting them in some way along the tenuous frontiers. Cêrn (modern Caduča) in particular experienced intermittent warfare for the better part of a century of Earth years. Ancurāna would often have competing interests with the Xšaŧa Empire to the north beyond the Šanrāxu River, over the statuses of Êîlur and Salenuž.

The Northern Wars would take place for a few decades starting in 705 AR, where Azuan tribes began to settle and develop by proxy of the Xšaŧans. However, soon after the Golden Years would ensue, where Ancurāna and Xšaŧa would both reach their zeniths simultaneously and a long–standing peace would be established. (654–454 AR). This era would come to an end with the assassination of an Ancu Emperor while touring Azua.

Unrest and internal strife would lead to the events of Fečāna and Ţíazrenu, sparking the Sâiýme organization. Ancurāna converted to [[Sâiýme]] in 389 AR, in the Great Conversion War. See the history of Sâiýme for more details.

Ancurāna sent delegations, along with Brilnaxa and Xšaŧa, to the Low Felari of Merŋulêam. This discovery would prompt the High Felari to intervene, and each group warned them of the other. Felari interference started in Ancu affairs in 360 AR.

Asxiýr in particular took an interest in Nulâašilan affairs, most especially Xšaŧa. Ancurāna helped assist Asxiýr's influence when Xšaŧa nearly erupted into civil war, solidifying them as an ally when the dust settled. In 206 AR Ancurāna, Xšaŧa, and Brilnaxa (all under High Felari sway) form the Pact of Ŋašilu against Anselesna, with rival High Felari aiming to either bolster the pact or to tear it asunder. 

The Wars of Wings during the AV years would greatly sap the strength of the empire; while not directly invaded nor near the front lines, the assumption of High Felari dominance greatly disrupted the empire and many were shipped off to war in the north. Anselesnan influence made it all the way to western [[Nulâašilu]], where tribes raided the western fringes of the empire with unusual ferocity. 

The Ancu Interregnum (351–428) was the closest Ancurāna got to disintegrating in the war. The Ancu Interregnum saw Xšaŧa intervene, and try to support a candidate that would be more favourable and less hostile to Xšaŧan lands; at the time, vassals of Ancurāna would often raid Xšaŧan lands and the emperors turn a blind eye, thus Xšaŧa in turn tried to reclaim their ancient stronghold in Castarŧ, sparking the 2nd War of the River. When the Interregnum ended, Ancurāna promised that for the return of Castarŧ they would enforce a permanent status of inviolability for Xšaŧan lands by their vassals, and permanent alliance. With the governor imposed by Xšaŧa on Castarŧ being unpopular Xšaŧa decided to relent, and the Treatise of Setec (now known as Seŧeu, in Salenuž) formed the Nulâašilan Alliance.

Ancurāna helped in the Siege of Xţām, where the capital of Xšaŧa was surrounded and besieged before being liberated with the help of Cí'Ilyān harrying the supply ships of Anselesna. Qiriýtaq would attempt to subvert the Ancu emperors who were solidly on the side of Asxiýr, but to no avail. The Little Interlude would last between 726-910 AV, between the liberation of Telcensā and the War of Brothers in Ancurāna.

Over time during the wars the empire would stagnate from within, losing the technological advantages it had once enjoyed The War of Brothers (913-920 AV) greatly shook the realm, as Qiriýtaq and Asxiýr vied for influence in the empire. Qiriýtaq appealed to long–overlooked nobles, whose deep discontentment with the wars to the north and the emperor sidelining their concerns led to their revolt. Xšaŧa and Asxiýr tried to intervene, but Qiriýtaq was ultimately successful.

The emperor at the time, Inare VI, knew that the empire's tenacity had come to an end. He gathered the council, and proclaimed that Ancurāna would be decentralized into 5 realms, each subordinate to Dānura in foreign affairs, taxes, and military spending; but otherwise with great autonomy. Nothing less was accepted by the victorious noble revolt, who still wanted an emperor because they know it would spark even further conflict if they dissolved the realm completely. The Ancu realms had (more-or-less) the same borders with each other that they do now, with the exception that Dānura also held the coastal lands and cities between the Ţānu to Nuvê Rivers, all islands in the Blue Sea, the forts on the Strait of Dāvi, and the cities of Epacre, Isim, and Strāleφ. 

Ultimately, this territorial division would do little for the ailing empire, as in 1012 AV the weakening grasp of Dānura over the foreign affairs and military spending of the 5 realms would see the 5 realms view Dānura with contempt, and desire - and start secret negotiations. Desire for the Catena, and the city of Ráemar. The pro-emperor faction at the time had largely been reduced to Dānura itself, which enabled this move as Qiriýtaq subtly encouraged the nobles of the 5 Realms. In 1016, the 5 lords at the time decided to unite, and throw off the yoke of Dānura. They invaded the island and Dānura's other lands, and captured the city. The continental possessions of Dānura would be partitioned between the 5 Realms, as would the minor Blue Sea islands. The island of Dānura would become a condominium between the 5, governed on behalf of all 5 by a weak local government. The 5 lords swore that they and their legal successors, by Sâî, must uphold their vows of peace and cooperation with each other, and to come to each other's aid should the existence of any of the 5 come under peril. With the Treaty of the 5 Lords signed in 1017, Ancurāna officially was no more.

Castarŧ and the eastern fringes of the empire were more loyal to the war effort than the 5 Realms proper, and as such a 6th Realm - Castarŧ - was also formed. It was not viewed officially by the other 5, and claimed by both Fiŷážca and Iŋatra. Xšaŧa supported Castarŧ, and viewed it as the remnant of the old Ancu government which was still loyal to the alliance. 

The 5 official Realms, meanwhile, largely withdrew from the Nulâašilan alliance in all but name. Asxiýr moved on from Ancurāna, seeing his influence there as a lost cause. Rather he moved to destabilize Qiriýtaq's influence in Tes Gaţel, the first realm to fall to Anselesna's forces.

# Government
Ancurāna was a monarchy using absolute primogeniture, but with mechanisms to remove a monarch should they be deemed not capable to rule the empire. There was a unicameral legislature, mostly subject to the emperor. The emperor could not, notably, remove members of the council.

One of Ancurāna's greatest prides is that it never, ever let one enemy reach the shores of the Blue Sea. The only exception would be in civil wars - internal threats. External enemies, however, have not once so much as even threatened the capital of Ráemar. This pride continues into the current day, where not even during the darkest time of the Ancu Realms (the Rítevan War, when Haxīr annexed around 25% of Mezāvca) did any enemy reach those shores.

# Supranational Ancurāna
While the Treaty of the 5 Lords dissolved the empire, Ancurāna is still a commonly used term to describe the cultural and geopolitical sphere of the former country. In particular, the foreign policy of the 5 Ancu Realms is often commonly aligned, and many analysts have taken to referring to the councils they hold in Ráemar as the informal formation of a supranational alliance and general co–operation comprising a new Ancurāna. 

However, it is also acceptable to merely say "The Ancu Realms" translatable to "d'Ancuhēŧye" which is an increasingly common term.
